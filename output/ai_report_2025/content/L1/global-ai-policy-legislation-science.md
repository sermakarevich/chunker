# global-ai-policy-legislation-science

**Parent:** [[content/L2/global-ai-policy-and-scientific-application|global-ai-policy-and-scientific-application]] — Between 2014 and 2024, the US Congress introduced 133 AI-related bills, while 35 states did so in 2023, complementing the EU's risk-based AI Act (categorizing systems into unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal risk) and China's specific regulations on recommendation algorithms and deep synthesis.

The global landscape of AI policy, legislation, and scientific application is characterized by a shift toward formal regulation and the strategic integration of AI into specialized scientific domains. In the United States, the regulatory environment is marked by a distinct contrast between federal and state-level activity. While federal legislative progress has been slower, state-level activity has been highly active. Between 2014 and 2024, the U.S. Congress introduced a total of 133 bills related to AI, while in 2023 alone, 35 U.S. states introduced AI-related bills. The U.S. focus has been heavily weighted toward AI safety, regulation, and the specific impacts of AI on labor and copyright laws.

In the European Union, the EU AI Act represents a primary regulatory pillar, adopting a risk-based approach to ensure safety and the protection of fundamental rights. This framework categorizes AI systems into four risk levels: unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal. Under this act, strict obligations are imposed on systems designated as 'high-risk,' requiring them to meet rigorous safety and transparency standards before deployment.

In China, regulatory efforts have taken a more application-specific approach. Rather than a broad horizontal framework, Chinese regulation has focused on particular technologies such as recommendation algorithms and 'deep synthesis.' Providers of these technologies are required to ensure that the content generated is truthful and that users are explicitly notified when they are interacting with with an AI system.

Beyond these major jurisdictions, Canada and other global regions have also engaged in efforts to balance the pursuit of technological innovation with the necessity of implementing safeguards to mitigate associated risks. This ongoing tension between fostering innovation and ensuring safety remains a central theme in global AI governance throughout 2024.

Parallel to these legislative developments, AI is being increasingly applied to accelerate scientific discovery. In the realm of materials science and drug discovery, AI is utilized to predict molecular properties and optimize designs, significantly reducing the time required for traditional laboratory experimentation. AI's role has also expanded into climate modeling, where it is used to analyze complex atmospheric patterns and improve the accuracy of environmental predictions. Additionally, the development of autonomous systems continues to be a priority, though this is coupled with an intensifying focus on AI safety and alignment. This alignment work is critical to ensure that as AI systems become more autonomous and capable, they remain beneficial to humanity and operate within the intended ethical and safety boundaries.
