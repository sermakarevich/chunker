# ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025

**Parent:** [[content/L2/ai-technical-performance-2025|ai-technical-performance-2025]] — The 2025 AI Index Report analyzes the convergence of frontier models, noting that o3 achieved an 87.5% score on ARC-AGI and o1 solved 74.4% of IMO qualifying exams, while the gap between US and Chinese models narrowed to 1.7% on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides an extensive analysis of AI benchmarks and model capabilities across language, vision, video, speech, and coding. 

### Language and Reasoning

In the domain of language, several new benchmarks and reasoning paradigms have emerged. The Arena-Hard-Auto benchmark, which leverages an automated pipeline called BenchBuilder to curate 500 challenging queries from the Chatbot Arena, uses GPT-4 Turbo as a judge against a baseline model (GPT-4-0314). More than 50% of its questions focus on coding and debugging. As of November 2024, the top scores on Arena-Hard-Auto were o1-mini (92.0), o1-preview (90.4), and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (85.2). Additionally, the benchmark features a style control leaderboard to assess how response style influences preference; the November variant of Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 3.5 is the top model on this specific leaderboard.

WildBench, developed in 2024 by researchers from the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington, addresses the limitations of academic-focused benchmarks like MMLU and human-heavy benchmarks like LMSYS by using diverse "in the wild" questions from over 1 million human-chatbot interactions. Currently, GPT-4o is the top-performing model on WildBench with an Elo score of 1227.1, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet with a score of 1215.4.

MixEval, launched by researchers from the National University of Singapore, Carnegie Mellon University, and the Allen Institute for AI, combines real-world user queries with ground-truth-based questions. On the MixEval-Hard suite, OpenAI’s o1-preview achieved the highest score of 72.0, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet-0620 (68.1) and Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct (66.2), all released in 2024.

OpenAI's o1 and o3 models represent a shift toward advanced reasoning through inference or test-time computation, utilizing a chain-of-thought process to break complex problems into smaller steps and iteratively check answers. This approach allows o1 to provide more thorough reasoning than GPT-4 when deciphering scrambled text. In head-to-head comparisons, o1 outperforms GPT-4o by 2.8 points on MMLU, 34.5 points on MATH, 26.7 points on GPQA Diamond, and 65.1 points on AIME 2024 (where o1 scored 74.4% vs GPT-4o's 9.3%). Furthermore, the o3 model achieved an 87.5% accuracy rate on the ARC-AGI benchmark, surpassing the previous record of 55.5%. 

However, these reasoning capabilities come with higher costs and latency. GPT-4o costs $2.50 per 1 million input tokens and $10 per 1 million output tokens, while o1 costs $15 per 1 million input tokens and $60 per 1 million output tokens. Additionally, o1 is approximately 40 times slower than GPT-4o, with a time to first token of 29.7 seconds compared to 0.72 seconds for GPT-4o. OpenAI released o1-preview to ChatGPT Plus and Teams users on September 12, 2024, and the full version of o1 and the $200 monthly ChatGPT Pro subscription on December 5, 2024. The o3 model remains restricted to select researchers and developers via a safety testing program.

### RAG, Embeddings, and Long-Context Retrieval

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) capabilities have evolved, with Anthropic introducing Contextual Retrieval in September 2024 to enhance these processes. New benchmarks for RAG include Ragnarok, CRAG, and FinanceBench for financial question answering. 

The Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB), created by Hugging Face and Cohere in late 2022, evaluates how models convert data into numerical embedding vectors across 58 datasets, 112 languages, and eight tasks: bitext mining, classification, clustering, pair classification, reranking, retrieval, semantic textual similarity, and summarization. As of early 2025, the top-performing embedding model is Voyage AI’s voyage-3-m-exp (score of 74.03), which uses quantization-aware training and Matryoshka Representation Learning. This model narrowly beat NV-Embed-v2, which held the top spot for most of 2024 with a score of 72.31. The leading model's score has risen from 59.5 in late 2022 to the current high.

Regarding long-context retrieval, models have shifted from simple "needle-in-the-haystack" tests to more complex evaluations. Context windows have expanded from 2023 models like GPT-4 and Llama 2 (8,000 and 4,000 tokens, respectively) to modern models like GPT-4o (May 2024) and Gemini 2.0 Pro Experimental (February 2025), which range from 128 thousand to 2 million tokens. 

To evaluate these expanded windows, Nvidia’s RULER benchmark assesses multihop reasoning, aggregation, and retrieval performance. Gemini-1.5-Pro achieved the highest weighted performance average of 95.5 on RULER, followed by GPT-4 (89.0) and GLM4 (88.0). RULER also found that only half of the popular LLMs claiming context sizes of 32K tokens or greater can actually maintain satisfactory performance at that length. Similarly, HELMET (How to Evaluate Long-Context Models Effectively and Thoroughly), a collaboration between Intel and Princeton, uses seven categories including passage re-ranking and synthetic recall. On HELMET (across 8K, 32K, and 128K settings), Gemini 1.5 Pro and the August variant of GPT-4 maintained effectiveness, while GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Llama 3.1-70B showed performance degradation.

### Computer Vision and Image/Video Generation

In computer vision, benchmarks have moved from simple classification (ImageNet) to complex reasoning. The Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) challenge (2019) tests the ability to provide rationales for answers. In July 2024, a model reached a Q->AR score of 85.0, matching the human benchmark and improving 4.2% over 2023. For video understanding, the MVBench benchmark (2023) requires temporal reasoning. As of 2024, the top model is Video-CCAM-7B-v1.2 (based on Qwen 2.5-7B-Instruct) with a score of 69.23, a 14.6% improvement since late 2023.

Image generation has progressed to the point where human faces are frequently indistinguishable from real photos. Midjourney's evolution from 2022 to 2025 shows a shift from cartoonish renderings to startlingly realistic depictions. Public preference is measured via the Chatbot Arena's Vision Arena, which has over 150,000 votes on prompts like "Batman drinking a coffee." As of early 2025, the top-ranked model is Google’s Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking-Exp-1219, with only a 3.4% gap between it and the fourth-ranked ChatGPT-4o-latest (2024-1120).

Video generation saw a significant leap in 2024. Stability AI launched Stable Video Diffusion (November 2023) and Stable Video 3D (March 2024), the latter generating 3D views from a single image. OpenAI previewed Sora in February 2024 and released it in December 2024, capable of 1080p 20-second videos. Meta unveiled Movie Gen in October 2024, which creates 16-second 1080p videos at 16 fps, incorporating sound and personalized video generation. Google launched Veo (May 2024) and Veo 2 (December 2024); internal benchmarks showed Veo 2 outperforming Movie Gen, Kling v1.5, and Sora Turbo. Other notable generators include Runway’s Gen-3 Alpha, Luma’s Dream Machine, and Kuaishou’s Kling 1.5. Improvement is evidenced by the "Will Smith eating spaghetti" prompt, where Pika's 2025 versions are vastly superior to 2023 versions.

### Speech and Coding

Speech recognition is evaluated on the LRS2 (Oxford-BBC Lip Reading Sentences 2) benchmark (2017), using Word Error Rate (WER). The model Whisper-Flamingo achieved a new standard with a WER of 1.3%, though the report suggests this benchmark may be nearing saturation.

Coding capabilities are evaluated through complex challenges as foundational benchmarks like HumanEval (164 problems) are now saturated—Claude 3.5 Sonnet (HPT) achieved a score of 100%. SWE-bench (October 2023), using 2,294 GitHub problems, shows a dramatic rise in performance: while the best model at the end of 2023 scored 4.4%, OpenAI’s o3 solved 71.7% by early 2025. On BigCodeBench (2024), which tests functions across 139 libraries, the best model, OpenAI’s o1, scored 35.5 on the hard subset. On the Chatbot Arena LLM leaderboard's coding filter, Gemini-Exp-1206 leads with a score of 1,369, followed by o1 at 1,361.
