Shifting to technical data engineering workflows and classification protocols, the data processing and classification methodology for AI procurement analysis followed a multistep approach. Initially, a 2023 study by the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) revealed that 49 agencies, including 25 executive branch agencies, failed to report data to USAspending, representing over $5 billion in net outlays for fiscal year 2022. To identify AI-related procurement, regular expression (regex) matching was applied to contract and grant awards, categorizing documents as either “non AI-related” or “AI-related.” To validate these matches, a BERTopic model and a pretrained DeBERTA transformer were employed to assess probability scores for specific AI-related topics. Procurement awards with relevance scores below 20% underwent manual review, while those with higher scores were confirmed as AI-related. To guarantee additional accuracy, all high-value tenders were also manually reviewed. Processing data from the TED (Tenders Electronic Daily) database presented significant challenges due to inconsistent contract description storage. Contract descriptions varied in XML tag names based on release time and procurement type. Some files contained aggregated descriptions while others detailed each awarded contract lot separately. To capture comprehensive information, the main descriptions of each competition call were combined with partial descriptions when available. The linguistic diversity across data from different countries required translating all texts into English using the deeptranslator tool and the Google Translator engine. Following translation, the tender texts were processed using natural language processing (NLP) techniques. These preprocessing steps included the removal of stop words and special characters, part-of-speech (POS) tagging to preserve key grammatical categories, conversion to lowercase, lemmatization, and the replacement of all numerical measures with a <NUM> tag.

Regarding educational data sourcing, Appendix 2 of the State of Computer Science Education 2024 report details the methodology used by Code.org, CSTA, and ECEP Alliance. The underlying dataset was compiled by the Raspberry Pi Computing Education Research Centre at the University of Cambridge, which expanded on Brookings Institution research from its 2021 report, *Building Skills for Life: How to Expand and Improve Computer Science Education Around the World*. One methodological adjustment was made to clarify that computer science in the United States is available as an elective course in some schools and districts, but not everywhere. AP Computer Science data is supplied to Code.org under an agreement between the College Board and Code.org, drawn directly from the College Board’s national and state summary reports. Access to computer science education data originates from Code.org, CSTA, and ECEP Alliance’s State of Computer Science Education 2024 report; researchers or data users can request access to the dataset by contacting membership@csteachers.org. Postsecondary completion data comes from the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System (IPEDS), which consolidates annual surveys by the U.S. Department of Education’s National Center for Education Statistics (NCES). IPEDS collects information from every participating college, university, and technical/vocational institution that receives federal student financial aid. Graduates in AI-related fields were identified using the Completions survey and classified according to the Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) codes: Computer and Information Sciences, General (11.01); Computer Programming (11.02); and Computer Science (11.07). The count of AI-related graduates in this report differs from prior years because the Computer Science Teachers Association (CSTA) and the Institute for Advancing Computing Education (IACE) published a State Standards Comparison report in December 2024. This dataset contains approximately 10,000 state-adopted K–12 standards and is available as both a spreadsheet and a Python notebook for analysis. Standards adopted by Colorado and Virginia in late 2024 are excluded from this dataset.

Higher education enrollment and graduate data were sourced from the OECD Data Explorer, specifically the table titled “Number of enrolled students, graduates and new entrants by field of education.” The methodology for this OECD dataset is documented in *Education at a Glance 2024: Sources, Methodologies and Technical Notes*.
