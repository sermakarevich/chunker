# ai-robotics-autonomous-motion-performance

**Parent:** [[content/L2/ai-technical-performance-2025-2|ai-technical-performance-2025-2]] — The AI Index Report 2025 Technical Performance chapter details record-breaking AI reasoning (OpenAI o3's 87.7% on GPQA Diamond) and robotics (Waymo's 150,000 weekly paid rides), while analyzing the AI Incident Database's record 233 incidents in 2024.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides a comprehensive analysis of advancements in AI reasoning, general-purpose assistants, robotics, and autonomous motion. 

Regarding general AI assistants, Meta introduced the GAIA benchmark in May 2024 to assess the ability of AI systems to perform a broad range of tasks, including reasoning, multimodal processing, web browsing, and tool use. GAIA consists of 466 questions that challenge models with complex, multistep problems requiring open web searches, the interpretation of multimodal inputs, and reasoning through intricate scenarios. When GAIA was first launched, Large Language Models (LLMs) significantly lagged behind human performance; for instance, GPT-4 with plugins correctly answered only 15% of the questions, while human respondents scored 92%. By 2024, the top system on GAIA reached a score of 65.1%, representing a roughly 30 percentage point increase over the highest score recorded in 2023.

In the field of robotics, the Robot Learning Benchmark (RLBench), launched in 2019, is one of the most widely adopted benchmarks and features 100 unique tasks ranging from simple target reaching to opening an oven and placing a tray inside. Evaluation typically focuses on a standardized subset of 18 tasks. As of January 2025, the top-performing model on this subset is SAM2Act—a collaboration between the University of Washington, Universidad Católica San Pablo, Nvidia, and the Allen Institute for AI—which achieved an 86.8% success rate. This is a 2.8 percentage point improvement over the 2024 state-of-the-art and a 66.7 percentage point increase from the leading score in 2021.

Humanoid robotics saw significant growth in 2024. Figure AI launched Figure 02, a general-purpose humanoid robot standing 5 feet 6 inches tall, weighing 154 pounds, and capable of handling a 44-pound payload. Figure 02 can operate for up to five hours on a single charge and can perform tasks such as making coffee and assisting in automotive assembly by placing sheet metal into a car fixture. Integrated with OpenAI, Figure 02 can engage in speech-to-speech reasoning to explain its actions and respond to queries. Other notable humanoid robots include Tesla’s Optimus (first launched in 2002 and redesigned in 2023) and Boston Dynamics’ Atlas.

Google DeepMind has also made significant strides in robotic advancements. In 2023, DeepMind launched PaLM-E and RT-2, which utilized transformer-based architectures and training on both manipulation and language data to excel at both robotic manipulation and text generation. In 2024, DeepMind introduced AutoRT, an AI system that uses foundation models to autonomously generate training data by coordinating multiple video-equipped robots, resulting in a dataset of 77,000 robotic trials across 6,650 unique tasks. DeepMind also developed SARA-RT, which uses "up-training" to convert the quadratic complexity of standard transformers into a linear model, reducing computational demands while maintaining performance. Other DeepMind contributions include the ALOHA (Autonomous and DemoStart Learning of High-level Activities) approach, allowing robots to perform intricate tasks such as tying shoelaces, hanging a shirt, and stacking kitchen items, and DemoStart, an auto-curriculum reinforcement learning method for mastering complex behaviors using sparse rewards and limited demonstrations. Furthermore, DeepMind introduced a robotic model in 2024 capable of reaching amateur human-level performance in competitive table tennis.

There was a significant push toward robotic foundation models in 2024. Nvidia introduced GR00T (Generalist Robot 00 Technology), a general-purpose foundation model for humanoid robots designed to mimic human movements and understand natural language, accompanied by data pipelines, simulation frameworks, and the Thor robotics computer. Other robotic foundation models include Covariant's RFM-1, which features language reasoning and real-world maneuverability, and LLaRA, developed by researchers at Stony Brook University and the University of Wisconsin-Madison, which integrates perception, communication, and action into a single deep learning model. These models build upon the 2023 trend of RT-2, PaLM-E, and Open-X Embodiment.

Regarding self-driving cars, Waymo (a subsidiary of Alphabet) operates robotaxis in Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Austin. As of January 2025, Waymo provides 150,000 paid rides per week, covering over a million miles. Through September 2024, Waymo recorded the following rider-only miles: 20.823 million in Phoenix, 10.209 million in San Francisco, 1.947 million in Los Angeles, and 124,000 in Austin. Waymo also plans to test vehicles in 10 additional cities, including Las Vegas, San Diego, and Miami, and has tested in snowy conditions in upstate New York and Truckee, California. 

In China, Baidu’s Apollo Go reported 988,000 rides in Q3 2024, a 20% year-over-year increase. As of October 2024, Apollo Go operated 400 robotaxis with plans to expand to 1,000 by the end of 2025. Pony.AI also plans to scale its fleet from 200 to between 2,000 and 3,000 vehicles by the end of 2026. China is currently testing more driverless cars than any other country and is rolling them out across 16 cities. In Europe, startups like Wayve are also gaining traction.

Tesla unveiled the Cybercab in October 2024, a two-passenger autonomous vehicle without a steering wheel or pedals, expected to enter production in 2026 at a price under $30,000. Tesla also unveiled the Robovan, an electric autonomous van capable of transporting up to 20 passengers. In China, Baidu's Apollo Go launched the RT6 robotaxi, which features a battery-swapping system and a price the price tag of $30,000. Additionally, Uber has partnered with WeRide—the first publicly listed robotaxi company—to develop an autonomous ride-sharing platform in Abu Dhabi.

In the realm of autonomous freight, companies like Kodiak have completed their first driverless deliveries. Aurora has reported over 1 million miles of autonomous freight hauling on U.S. highways since 2021, though these miles were completed with human safety drivers present. Aurora has delayed the commercial launch of its fleet from the end of 2024 to April 2025.

To evaluate these capabilities, new benchmarks were introduced in 2024. NuPlan, developed by Motional, is a large-scale autonomous driving dataset and evaluation framework for testing machine-learning-based motion planners, featuring 1,282 hours of diverse driving scenarios and a training set of over 2 million fully annotated frames from more than 10,000 clips. Bench2Drive provides a comprehensive, realistic, closed-loop testing simulation environment for end-to-end autonomous vehicles to address the limitations of open-loop evaluations. According to Bench2Drive, top performers include TCP* (64.22), DriveAdapter (49.30), and ThinkTwice (40.73). OpenAD is the first real-world, open-world autonomous driving benchmark for 3D object detection, focusing on domain generalization and open-vocabulary recognition.

Safety data suggests that self-driving cars may be safer than human drivers. Waymo data for Phoenix and San Francisco shows significantly fewer incidents per million miles than human benchmarks: 0.32 airbag deployments compared to 5.91 for humans, 0.90 injury-reported crashes compared to 40.06 for humans, and 1.74 police-reported incidents compared to 2.26 for humans. Specifically, the percentage difference to the human benchmark is -88% for airbag deployments in Phoenix and -87% in San Francisco, -78% for injury-reported crashes in Phoenix and -78% in San Francisco, and -77% for police-reported incidents in Phoenix and -88% in San Francisco. 

Furthermore, a study by Waymo and Swiss Re analyzed liability claims across 25.3 million miles driven. The study found that Waymo vehicles had an 88% reduction in property damage claims and a 92% reduction in bodily injury claims compared to human-driver baselines. In real terms, Waymo vehicles were involved in only nine property damage claims and two bodily injury claims over that distance, whereas human drivers would be expected to incur 78 property damage claims and 26 bodily injury claims.
