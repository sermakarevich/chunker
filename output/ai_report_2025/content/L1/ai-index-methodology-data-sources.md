# ai-index-methodology-data-sources

**Parent:** [[index]]

The analysis of Artificial Intelligence (AI) legislation, policy, and educational integration is conducted through a multi-faceted methodology involving diverse data sources and technical analysis techniques. To track legislation and policy at both the federal and state levels, researchers utilize the US Federal Register and the 'Legiscan' API to monitor activity. The technical process of analyzing these legislative documents involves the use of Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, such as keyword extraction and topic modeling (Latent Dirichlet Allocation or LDA), to categorize bills based on specific themes including 'AI ethics,' 'automation,' and 'algorithmic transparency.'

In the realm of education, the methodology focuses on the integration of AI into school curricula. This is achieved by examining the 'Common Sense Education' AI curriculum and reviewing various university-level AI course offerings. To understand the policy landscape surrounding AI in education, researchers review governmental reports from the US Department of Education and the OECD. 

Specifically, the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025 provides a detailed breakdown of its data sources and methodologies for education and public opinion. To track graduates in AI-related fields, the report employs the Completions survey, which monitors students completing postsecondary education programs. Graduates are identified based on their first major according to the Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) codes: Computer and Information Sciences, General (11.01), Computer Programming (11.02), and Computer Science (11.07).

Regarding K-12 education, the 2025 AI Index's methodology for counting graduates differs from previous years due to a collaboration with the Computer Science Teachers Association (CSTA) and the Institute for Advancing Computing Education (IACE). This collaboration resulted in the publication of a State Standards Comparison report in December 2024, which includes a dataset of approximately 10,000 state-adopted K-12 standards. This dataset is available as a spreadsheet and a Python notebook. However, it is noted that the standards for Colorado and Virginia are excluded from this dataset because they were adopted in late 2024.

For broader educational data, the report utilizes the OECD Data Explorer, specifically the table 'Number of enrolled students, graduates and new entrants by field of education.' The specific methodology for this data is detailed in the 'Education at a Glance 2024 Sources, Methodologies and Technical Notes.'

Finally, the analysis of public opinion, detailed in Chapter 8 of the 2025 AI Index, is based on a survey conducted by Ipsos. While the report does not republish the full Ipsos survey methodology for brevity, it directs readers to the with the original survey for more detailed information.

This comprehensive approach integrates legislative tracking via the Federal Register and Legiscan, technical NLP analysis of bills, and a detailed educational pipeline analysis using CIP codes (11.01, 11.02, 11.07) and OECD data, while simultaneously monitoring public sentiment via Ipsos polling and K-12 standards via CSTA and IACE datasets containing 10,000 standards excluding Colorado and Virginia.
