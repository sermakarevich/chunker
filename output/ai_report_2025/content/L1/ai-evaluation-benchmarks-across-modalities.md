# ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities

**Parent:** [[content/L2/ai-index-2025-model-convergence-and-evaluation-landscape|ai-index-2025-model-convergence-and-evaluation-landscape]] — The AI Index Report 2025 documents a period of rapid market convergence and technical scaling, where U.S. private AI investment reached $109.1 billion, model performance gaps between open/closed weights and U.S./Chinese systems collapsed to under 2%, and benchmarks like ARC-AGI saw o3 achieve 87.5% while Waymo's autonomous fleet logged 25.3 million miles with a 92% reduction in injury claims compared to human baselines.

The evaluation landscape for artificial intelligence has expanded significantly across modalities, with specialized frameworks emerging to track progress in embeddings, long-context processing, vision, video, speech, coding, mathematics, and general reasoning. For text embeddings, the Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB), developed by Hugging Face and Cohere and introduced in late 2022, comprehensively evaluates how models convert words, texts, or documents into numerical vectors for semantic retrieval. MTEB spans 58 datasets across 112 languages and covers eight embedding tasks: bitext mining, classification, clustering, pair classification, reranking, retrieval, semantic textual similarity, and summarization, where bitext mining specifically matches sentences across different languages. As of early 2025, Voyage AI’s voyage-3-m-exp leads the MTEB leaderboard with a score of 74.03. Specializing in high-quality AI embeddings, Voyage AI designed this variant of the voyage-3-large foundation model using Matryoshka Representation Learning and quantization-aware training to narrowly outperform NV-Embed-v2, which scored 72.31 and held the top position for most of 2024. Since MTEB's launch, leading models have demonstrated meaningful performance improvements, with the initial late 2022 leader achieving an average score of 59.5.

Concurrently, evaluating long-context capabilities has become critical as large language models expanded context windows from 4,000 to 8,000 tokens (exemplified by 2023 models like GPT-4 and Llama 2) to 128,000 to 2 million tokens (seen in May 2024 models like GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Pro Experimental). Nvidia introduced the RULER benchmark in 2024 to move beyond limited needle-in-the-haystack scenarios, assessing retrieval, multihop reasoning, aggregation, and question answering. February 2025 results placed Gemini-1.5-Pro at the top with a weighted performance average of 95.5, followed by GPT-4 at 89.0 and GLM4 at 88.0. RULER researchers found that many models claiming context sizes of 32,000 tokens or greater fail to maintain satisfactory performance at that length, revealing operational context windows shorter than advertised. Complementing this, HELMET, introduced in 2024 through an Intel and Princeton University collaboration, addresses prior shortcomings like insufficient downstream task coverage and unreliable metrics. HELMET features seven long-context evaluation categories and tests models across 8,000, 32,000, and 128,000 context settings. While models including GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Llama 3.1-70B experience performance degradation in longer settings, Gemini 1.5 Pro and the August variant of GPT-4 maintain their effectiveness.

In vision and video understanding, computer vision benchmarks have evolved from early ImageNet dependencies to complex reasoning tests. The Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) challenge, introduced in 2019 by University of Washington and Allen Institute for AI researchers, tests commonsense visual reasoning by requiring systems to answer questions based on images and justify their logic, measured via the Q->AR score. The human baseline for VCR is 85.0; in July 2024, a model matched this baseline, marking a 4.2% improvement since 2023. For temporal dynamics, MVBench, introduced in late 2023 by researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai AI Laboratory, University of Hong Kong, Fudan University, and Nanjing University, tests multimodal video understanding and temporal reasoning across multiple frames. As of 2024, Video-CCAM-7B-v1.2, built on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, leads MVBench with a score of 69.23, a 14.6% improvement since its introduction. Image generation has also advanced to the point where AI-generated human faces are nearly indistinguishable from photographs. Midjourney’s evolution from 2022 to 2025 demonstrates this shift vividly: early versions (V1 through V4) produced cartoonish and inaccurate renderings of the prompt “a hyper-realistic image of Harry Potter,” whereas V6.1 in 2025 generates startlingly realistic depictions. This progress tracks with the Chatbot Arena Vision leaderboard, which ranks over 50 vision models on user prompts like “Batman drinking a coffee.” By early 2025, Google’s Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking-Exp-1219 leads with more than 150,000 user votes, holding a mere 3.4% performance gap over fourth-ranked ChatGPT-4o-latest (released November 20, 2024).

Text-to-video generation accelerated rapidly in 2024, overcoming earlier limitations of low quality, silence, and short clips. Stability AI launched its first foundation model, Stable Video Diffusion, in November 2023, followed by Stable Video 3D in March 2024 for generating 3D views and videos from single images. OpenAI previewed Sora in March 2024, a diffusion-based model creating 20-second clips up to 1080p through progressive refinement, which became publicly accessible in December 2024. Meta introduced Movie Gen in October 2024, supporting instruction-based video editing, sound incorporation, and 16-second 1080p videos at 16 frames per second. Google launched Veo in May 2024 and Veo 2 in December 2024, with internal benchmarks showing Veo 2 consistently outperforming Movie Gen, Kling v1.5, and Sora Turbo in user preference tests. Runway’s Gen-3 Alpha, Luma’s Dream Machine, and Kuaishou’s Kling 1.5 also contributed to the landscape. Pika’s iterations on “Will Smith eating spaghetti” illustrate this trajectory, showing dramatic quality gains between December 2023 (V1.0) and February 2025 (V2.2). Speech recognition has similarly matured; the LRS2 dataset, introduced in 2017, remains the standard for lip-reading and automatic speech recognition using word error rate (WER). In 2024, Whisper-Flamingo achieved a state-of-the-art WER of 1.3% on LRS2, surpassing the 2023 record of 1.5%. Given the extremely low error margin, significant future improvements appear unlikely, suggesting benchmark saturation.

Coding benchmarks reflect similar saturation, prompting more complex evaluation suites. OpenAI’s HumanEval, introduced in 2021, tests coding through 164 handwritten programming problems. By early 2025, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (HPT) achieved a perfect 100% Pass@1 score. SWE-bench, launched in October 2023 by Princeton and University of Chicago researchers, evaluates software engineering using 2,294 real-world GitHub issues and Python repository problems, featuring Lite and Verified subsets. On the Verified set, performance surged from 4.4% at the end of 2023 to 71.7% for OpenAI’s o3 model in early 2025. To address multi-function invocations and natural-language coding instructions, an international team released BigCodeBench in 2024, requiring LLMs to invoke functions across 139 libraries and 7 domains in 1,140 fine-grained tasks; OpenAI’s o1 achieved an average Pass@1 score of 35.5 on its hard subset. Community perception is tracked via the Chatbot Arena Coding filter, where early 2025 data shows Gemini-Exp-1206 leading with an Elo score of 1,369, followed closely by OpenAI’s o1 at 1,361, and DeepSeek-V3 at 1,317, representing a 3.8% gap between first and third place.

Mathematical evaluation frameworks track progress from grade-school arithmetic to graduate-level abstract reasoning. GSM8K, introduced by OpenAI in 2021, contains approximately 8,000 grade-school math word problems requiring multistep arithmetic solutions. A Claude Sonnet 3.5 variant using HPT prompting achieved a 97.72% accuracy, a notable rise from 2023’s 91.00%, though 2024 scores from Mistral, Meta, and Qwen hovering around 96% indicate approaching saturation. The MATH dataset, introduced in 2021 by UC Berkeley and University of Chicago researchers, contains 12,500 competition-level problems. While AI initially solved only 6.9%, OpenAI’s o3-mini (high) solved 97.9% by January 2025, surpassing the 90% human baseline for the first time. The LMSYS Chatbot Arena math filter evaluates over 181 models on public preference, accumulating 340,000+ votes, with OpenAI’s o1 (released December 2024) ranking first. To tackle saturation, Epoch AI introduced FrontierMath, featuring hundreds of original, exceptionally challenging problems vetted by experts, often requiring hours or days to solve. It tests advanced mathematics including number theory, real analysis, algebraic geometry, and category theory. On FrontierMath, Gemini 1.5 Pro scored 2.0% among six leading LLMs, while OpenAI’s o3 scored 25.2%.

In theorem proving, DeepMind developed AlphaProof, a reinforcement learning system derived from AlphaZero that generates hypotheses verified via the Lean interactive proof system, aided by a fine-tuned Gemini model translating natural language into formal representations. Its sibling, AlphaGeometry 2, is a neuro-symbolic hybrid system trained on synthetic data. Prior to 2024, AlphaGeometry solved 83% of historical International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) geometry problems. The IMO, established in 1959, remains the world’s oldest and most prestigious youth mathematics competition. During the 2024 competition, AlphaGeometry solved the sole geometry problem in just 24 seconds. AlphaProof solved 25 out of 30 Olympiad geometry problems, surpassing the average IMO silver medalist score of 22.9, and solved two algebra and one number theory problem, but failed two combinatorics problems. Automatic theorem-proving systems are also evaluated on the TPTP benchmark, used since 1997 for software verification and logic. A 2024 update, TPTP v.9.0.0, contains over 25,000 problems; fully automatic systems now solve 89% of them. Cross-testing remains unfeasible due to differing logical frameworks and a lack of translators, and the library size is insufficient to train AlphaProof without synthetic examples.

General reasoning benchmarks assess logical conclusions across broad, multidisciplinary domains rather than narrow tasks. The MMMU benchmark, developed by US and Canadian researchers, uses approximately 11,500 college-level questions across six core disciplines: art and design, business, science, health and medicine, humanities and social science, and technology and engineering. It utilizes charts, maps, tables, and chemical structures. In January 2025, OpenAI’s o1 achieved a 78.2% score on MMMU, a significant jump from 59.4%, though it remains below the 82.6% medium human expert baseline. The GPQA benchmark, introduced in 2023 by NYU, Anthropic, and Meta, tests graduate-level general reasoning using 448 difficult multiple-choice questions in biology, physics, and chemistry that cannot be answered via web search. On the diamond subset, human experts scored 81.3%, but OpenAI’s o3 (launched December 2024) scored 87.7%, marking a 48.9 percentage point improvement over 2023’s state-of-the-art. Finally, the ARC-AGI benchmark addresses artificial general intelligence claims by testing systems' ability to generalize beyond prior training. It presents independent tasks with demonstration or input pairs followed by test or output scenarios, deliberately avoiding reliance on memorization or narrow pattern matching.
