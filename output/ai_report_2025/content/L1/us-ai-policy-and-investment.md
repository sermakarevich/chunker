# us-ai-policy-and-investment

**Parent:** [[content/L2/global-ai-policy-and-scientific-application|global-ai-policy-and-scientific-application]] — Between 2014 and 2024, the US Congress introduced 133 AI-related bills, while 35 states did so in 2023, complementing the EU's risk-based AI Act (categorizing systems into unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal risk) and China's specific regulations on recommendation algorithms and deep synthesis.

The landscape of Artificial Intelligence in the United States is characterized by a complex interplay of legislative efforts, federal policy, public spending, and educational integration. This multifaceted approach seeks to balance the pursuit of global competitiveness and innovation with the critical need for safety, ethics, and the protection of individual rights.

### US Policy, Legislation, and the Management of Deepfakes

Legislative efforts in the US are heavily focused on the creation and dissemination of deceptive media, specifically AI-generated content and deepfakes. Both state-level regulations and federal proposals are being implemented to manage these risks. The primary objective of these regulations is to enforce transparency, frequently requiring that AI-generated content be explicitly labeled to prevent deception. Furthermore, these laws aim to protect individuals' likenesses from unauthorized AI synthesis, ensuring that a person's image or voice cannot be cloned without permission.

Beyond individual protections, there is a significant legislative push to penalize the use of deepfakes in electoral contexts. This is seen as a critical measure to prevent voter manipulation and to protect the integrity of democratic processes from the influence of synthetic media that could mislead the electorate.

### Federal Regulation and Public Investment

On a federal level, the US government has integrated AI into various operational sectors, including healthcare and defense, while simultaneously establishing frameworks for its use within federal agencies. This deployment is accompanied by a a range of regulatory actions designed to manage the challenges and opportunities presented by AI adoption in government.

Financial investment in AI is a primary driver of US strategy. There has been a substantial increase in public funding for AI, which is distributed across both basic research and applied applications. This funding is intended to foster the development of new AI capabilities while ensuring that the US remains at the forefront of technological advancement. The scale of this investment is reflected in the distribution of funds for AI-related projects across various government departments, emphasizing the role of the US government as a central coordinator and financier of AI progress.

### Education, Computing, and Strategic Policy

There is a significant disparity in how AI and computing are approached across different sectors of the US economy and society. This is most evident in the realm of education, where there is a concerted effort to integrate computing into school curricula. However, the degree of this integration is inconsistent, varying widely across different regions and educational institutions.

From a strategic policy perspective, the US has undergone a shift toward regulating AI to ensure safety and ethics. This shift is intended to complement the push for innovation; the goal is to create a regulatory environment that encourages the development of a high-performance AI ecosystem while mitigating the risks of harm. This dual-track approach—combining heavy public investment in basic and applied research with targeted legislation against deepfakes and a broader movement toward ethical AI regulation—is designed to maintain the United States' terms of global competitiveness in the rapidly evolving AI landscape.
