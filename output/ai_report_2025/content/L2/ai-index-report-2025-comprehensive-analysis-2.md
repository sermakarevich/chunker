# ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis-2

**Parent:** [[index]]

The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published in April 2025 by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI (HAI), is the eighth edition of the report. This report is the most comprehensive to date, incorporating detailed analyses of AI hardware, inference costs, and trends in AI publication and patenting. It also expands its coverage of AI's role in in science and medicine, corporate adoption of responsible AI (RAI) practices, and global AI ecosystems. The AI Index was founded in 2017 as an offshoot of the One Hundred Year Study of Artificial Intelligence (AI100) to provide policymakers, journalists, researchers, and the public with rigorously validated, globally sourced data.

### Administration and Production

The writing process was assisted by AI tools, specifically ChatGPT and Claude, for copy editing and tightening initial human-written drafts. ### Overview and Global Trends

Co-directors Yolanda Gil and Raymond Perrault described 2024 as a pivotal year. AI's contribution to advancing knowledge was recognized by Nobel prizes in physics and chemistry, and the Turing award for reinforcement learning. The Turing Test is no longer considered an ambitious goal as sophisticated systems have surpassed it. Corporate investment rebounded in 2024, with newly funded generative AI startups nearly tripling, and business adoption of AI accelerated significantly.

Governments have increased their involvement, launching billion-dollar national AI infrastructure initiatives, including expanding energy capacity. However, public trust remains a challenge. A 2024 global survey showed a rise in optimism about AI's societal benefits, while fewer people believe AI companies safeguard own data, and concerns regarding fairness and bias persist.

### Chapter 1: Research and Development

#### AI Research Output and Influence

AI research is distributed geographically and by sector. In 2023, East Asia and the Pacific led in research output, accounting for 34.5% of all AI publications, and 37.1% of all citations. North America and Europe and Central Asia followed, with publications at 10.3% and citations at 18.2% publications. While North American and European shares of citations have declined since 2017, East Asia and the Pacific's share has risen sharply.

At the country level, China led in total AI publications in 2023 (23.2%) and citations (22.6%), while the United States led in highly influential research. The U.S. consistently produced the most top-cited (top 100) publications from 2021 to 2023: 64 in 2021, 59 in 2022, and 50 in 2023. Academia remains the leading producer of high-influence research, with 34 top-cited publications in 2021, 2022, and 2023 (34, 27, 42 respectively), while industry contributions to the top 100 dropped from 17 in 2021 and 19 in 2022 to 7 in 2023. By organization, Google led each year from 2021 to 2023, tying with Tsinghua University in 2023 when both contributed eight publications to the top 100. In 2023, Carnegie Mellon University was the highest-ranked U.S. academic institution.

#### AI Patents and Notable Models

AI patenting is increasing; from 2010 to 2023, granted AI patents grew from 3,833 to 122,511, with a 29.6% increase in the last year. China leads in total AI patents, accounting for 69.7% of all grants as of 2023, with 82.4% of granted AI patents originating from East Asia and the Pacific. North America accounted for 14.2% of grants. South Korea (17.27 per 100,000 inhabitants) and Luxembourg (15.31) are the top producers on a per capita basis in 2023. Between 2013 and 2023, Luxembourg (8,216%), China (6,317%), and Sweden (3,453%) saw the greatest percentage increase in per capita patenting.

Regarding "notable machine learning models," the United States led in 2024, producing 40 models, followed by China with 15 and France with three. Since 2003, the U.S. has produced more notable models than the UK, China, and Canada. In 2024, industry dominated model development, with nearly 90% (specifically 90.2%) of such models originating from industry, compared to 60% in 2023. Industry produced 55 notable AI models in 2024, and no notable AI models originated from academia in 2024. Over the past decade, Google has been the most prolific, producing 187 models, followed by Meta (82) and Microsoft (39). In 2024 specifically, top contributors were Google (7), OpenAI (7), and Alibaba (6).

#### Model Development, Training, and Access

Notable AI models are released under various access types: API access (most common in 2024, 32.8% of notable models), Hosted access (no API), Open weights (restricted use), Open weights (unrestricted), Open weights (noncommercial), and unreleased models. In 2024, the breakdown of release types was: API access (20 of 61 models, 32.8%), Open weights (restricted use) at 18.03%, Unreleased at 16.39%, Open weights (unrestricted) at 11.48%, Open weights (noncommercial) at 9.84%, Hosted access (no API) at 8.20%, and Unknown at 3.28%. Additionally, 60.7% of notable AI models in 2024 were launched without training code.

Model parameters and training data have scaled rapidly. Training dataset sizes for LLMs double approximately every eight months, and training compute for notable AI models doubles every five months. Power required for training doubles annually. The Transformer model (2017) was trained on 2 billion tokens, GPT-3 175B (davinci) on 374 billion tokens, and Meta’s Llama 3.3 (2024) on 15 trillion tokens. Training times have also increased: AlexNet (2012) took five to six days, Llama 3.1-405B took and took approximately 90 days, and Google’s Gemini 1.0 Ultra (2023) took around 100 days. Training compute requirements have grown from 470 petaFLOP for AlexNet (2012) to 38 billion petaFLOP for GPT-4o.

#### Compute Scaling, Data Bottlenecks, and Inference Costs

DeepSeek's V3 model (December 2024) achieved high performance with fewer resources than leading LLMs. However, top 10 Chinese language models have scaled their training compute at a rate of approximately three times per year since late 2021, which is slower than the global trend of five times per year since 2018. Top-tier U.S. models have generally been more computationally intensive than those from China.

There is a significant debate on the "data commons." The C4 common crawl dataset saw the proportion of restricted tokens jump from 5–7% to 20–33% in 2023–2024. In the C4 dataset, Common Crawl contains a median of 130 trillion tokens, and the indexed web holds 510 trillion tokens. Epoch AI projects that current training data stocks will be fully utilized between 2026 and 2032. a revised estimate from earlier projections that high-quality text data would be depleted by 2024. This is because web data performs better than curated corpora and models can be trained on the same datasets multiple times. 

Synthetic data is being used to mitigate data bottlenecks. While models trained on repeated cycles of synthetic data may experience "model collapse," synthetic data layered on top of real data avoids this. In healthcare, synthetic data has increased F1 scores or AUROC by 5%–10% on minority classes. To improve factuality, the FactTune-FS methods (Stanford and University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill) have outperformed other RLHF and decoding-based methods.

Inference costs for a system performing at the level of GPT-3.5 (64.8 on MMLU) dropped from $20.00 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 per million tokens in October 2024 (Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B), a more than 280-fold reduction. For models scoring above 50% on GPQA, costs declined from $15 per million tokens in May 2024 to $0.12 per million tokens in December 2024 (Phi 4). Epoch AI estimates that LLM inference prices have fallen between 9 and 900 times per year depending on the task.

#### AI Hardware and Environmental Impact

AI hardware performance (16-bit floating-point operations) has grown 43% annually, doubling every 1.9 years. Costs have dropped 30% per year, and energy efficiency has increased by 40% annually. However, carbon emissions from training have risen sharply: AlexNet (2012) emitted 0.01 tons of carbon, GPT-3 (2020) emitted 588 tons, GPT-4 (2023) emitted 5,184 tons, and Llama 3.1 405B (2024) emitted 8,930 tons. For comparison, the average American emits 18 tons of carbon per year.

### Chapter 2: Technical Performance

AI systems are mastering benchmarks faster than ever. In 2024, scores on MMMU, GPQA, and SWE-bench rose by 18.8, 48.9, and 67.3 percentage points, respectively, compared to 2023. On SWE-bench, AI systems improved from solving 4.4% of coding problems in 2023 to 71.7% in 2024.

Open-weight models are closing the gap with closed-weight models. In early January 2024, the leading closed-weight model outperformed the top open-weight model by 8.0% on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard; by February 2025, this gap narrowed to 1.7%.

Similarly, the performance gap between Chinese and U.S. models is narrowing. At the end of 2023, gaps on MMLU, MMMU, MATH, and HumanEval were 17.5, 13.5, 24.3, and 31.6 percentage points, respectively. By the end of 2024, these margins shrunk to 0.3, 8.1, 1.6, and 3.7 percentage points.

Frontier model performance is converging. The Elo score difference between the top and 10th-ranked model on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard fell from 11.9% to 5.4% in a year. The difference between the top two models shrank from 4.9% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024.

New reasoning paradigms, such as test-time compute (e.g., OpenAI's o1 and o3 models), have improved performance. The o1 model scored 74.4% on an International Mathematical Olympiad qualifying exam, { "//": "Comparison with GPT-4o's 9.3%" } compared to 9.3% for GPT-4o. However, o1 is nearly six times more expensive and 30 times slower than GPT-4o.

Due to the4 saturation of traditional benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval), new, more challenging evaluations are emerging. These include Humanity’s Last Exam (top system scores 8.80%), FrontierMath (AI solves only 2%), and BigCodeBench (AI systems achieve 35.5% success vs. human standard of 97%).

High-quality AI video generation saw significant gains in 2024 with the launch of SORA, Stable Video Diffusion 3D and 4D, Movie Gen, and Veo 2. Video generation and programming tasks are also areas of significant stride.

Performance gains are also being seen in smaller models. In 2022, the smallest model scoring over 60% on MMLU was PaLM (540 billion parameters). By 2024, Microsoft’s Phi-3-mini (3.8 billion parameters) achieved this, a 142-fold reduction in size.

Despite these advances, complex reasoning remains a challenge. LLMs still struggle with benchmarks like PlanBench and frequently fail logic tasks where correct solutions exist, even with chain-of-thought reasoning.

AI agents are showing early promise; in short time-horizon settings (two-hour budget), top systems score four times higher than human experts according to RE-Bench. according to RE-Bench. However, in 32-hour budget settings, human performance surpasses AI two to one.

### Chapter 3: Responsible AI (RAI)

Evaluation of AI systems using RAI criteria remains uncommon, though new benchmarks like HELM Safety, AIR-Bench, and FACTS are emerging to assess factuality and safety. Other factuality benchmarks include the updated Hughes Hallucination Evaluation Model leaderboard, SimpleQA, and the previous HaluEval and TruthfulQA.

AI-related incidents are rising. The AI Incidents Database reports that incidents rose to 233 in 2024, a 56.4% increase over 2023. The Foundation Model Transparency Index shows that average scores for major developers increased from 37% in October 2023 to 58% in May 2024.

Organizations acknowledge RAI risks, but mitigation efforts lag. A McKinsey survey shows top concerns are inaccuracy (64%), regulatory compliance (63%), and cybersecurity (60%), but not all are taking active steps.

Policymakers are increasingly involved. In 2024, the OECD, European Union, United Nations, and African Union published frameworks for the transparency, explainability, and trustworthiness of AI.

The "data commons" is shrinking. In the C4 common crawl dataset, the proportion of restricted tokens jumped from 5–7% to 20–33% between 2023 and 2024 as websites implement protocols to curb data scraping.

AI-related election misinformation spread globally in 2024 across more than a dozen countries and over 10 social media platforms, including the U.S. presidential election.

Implicit bias remains a problem. Models such as GPT-4 and Claude 3 Sonnet continue to associate negative terms with Black individuals, associate women with humanities rather than STEM, and favor men for leadership roles.

Academic research into RAI is growing, RAI papers accepted at leading conferences increased by 28.8% (from 992 in 2023 to 1,278 in 2024).

### Chapter 4: Economy

Global private AI investment reached a record $252.3 billion in 2024, a 26% increase. Private investment grew 44.5% and mergers and acquisitions (M&A) rose 12.1% from the previous year. Total investment has grown more than thirteenfold since 2014. Total investment has grown more than thirteenfold since 2014. 

Generative AI specifically attracted $33.9 billion in global private investment in 2024, an 18.7% increase from 2023. Generative AI now represents more than 20% of all AI-related private investment.

The United States leads significantly in private investment. In 2024, U.S. private AI investment reached $109.1 billion, nearly 12 times that of China ($9.3 billion) and 24 times that of the U.K. ($4.5 billion). In generative AI, the U.S. investment gap exceeded the combined total of China, the EU, and the U.K. by $25.4 billion in 2024, expanding from a $21.8 billion gap in 2023.

Organizational use of AI has surged. In 2024, 78% of organizations reported using AI, and use of generative AI in at least one business function rose from 33% in 2024 to 71% in 2024. (Note: The source text contains a 2024 to 2024 increase, but the provided data is listed as such).

AI is beginning to deliver financial impact. Among companies reporting financial impacts, 49% of those using AI in service operations, 43% in supply chain management, and 41% in software engineering report cost savings (though most report these savings are less than 10%). Regarding revenue, 71% of those using AI in marketing and sales, 63% in supply chain management, and 57% in service operations report revenue gains (（most report these increases are less than 5%).

Regional shifts in AI use are observed. While North America leads, Greater China saw one of the most significant growth rates, with a 27 percentage point increase. Europe followed with with a 23 percentage point increase.

China continues to dominate industrial robotics. In 2023, China installed 276,300 industrial robots, 6 times more than Japan and 7.3 times more than the United States. China's share of global installations has risen to 51.1% since 2013. ### Chapter 5: Science and Medicine

In 2024, high-performance protein sequencing models like ESM3 and AlphaFold 3 were launched. AI continues to drive scientific discovery through tools like Aviary (biological tasks) and FireSat (wildfire prediction). 

Clinical knowledge in LLMs is improving. OpenAI's o1 model achieved 96.0% on the MedQA benchmark, a 5.8 percentage point gain over the best 2023 score. Performance on MedQA has improved by 28.4 percentage points since late 2022.

AI has shown the ability to outperform doctors in specific clinical tasks, such as diagnosing complex clinical cases (GPT-4), cancer detection, and identifying high-mortality-risk patients. However, the best results are often achieved through AI-doctor collaboration.

FDA-approved, AI-enabled medical devices increased from six in 2015 to 223 by 2023.

Synthetic data is showing promise in medicine for identifying social determinants of health, discovering new drug compounds, and enhancing privacy-preserving clinical risk prediction. 

Medical foundation models were released in 2024, 2024, including multimodal models like Med-Gemini and specialized models for echocardiology (EchoCLIP), ophthalmology (VisionFM), and radiology (ChexAgent).

Public protein science databases have grown significantly since 2021, with entries increasing in AlphaFold (585%), PDB (23%), and UniProt (31%).

AI-driven research earned two Nobel Prizes in 2024: Demis Hassabis and John Jumper (Google DeepMind) for protein folding with AlphaFold (Chemistry), and John Hopfield and Geoffrey Hinton for neural networks (Physics).

### Chapter 6: Policy and Governance

U.S. states are leading AI legislation due to slow federal progress. State-level AI laws increased from one in 2016 to 49 by 2023, and 131 by 2024. U.S. federal agencies introduced 59 AI-related regulations in 2024, more than double the 25 regulations from 21 agencies in 2023.

Global governments are investing in AI infrastructure. Major investments include: 
- Canada: $2.4 billion
- China: $47.5 billion semiconductor fund
- France: €109 billion (committed) / $117 billion
- India: $1.25 billion
- Saudi Arabia: $100 billion (Project Transcendence)

Mentions of AI in legislative proceedings across 75 countries increased by 21.3% in 2024, rising from 1,557 to 1,889. Since 2016, total mentions have grown more than ninefold.

International AI safety institutes were launched in 20er 2024. Following the AI Seoul Summit in May 2024, institutes were pledged in Japan, France, Germany, Italy, Singapore, South Korea, Australia, Canada, and the EU, following the initial U.S. and U.K. institutes (Nov 2023).

U.S. states have expanded deepfake regulations. By 2024, 24 states had passed regulations, with 15 more (including New York, Oregon, and New Mexico) introducing measures in 2024.

### Chapter 7: Education

In the U.S., 81% of high school CS teachers agree that AI should be part of the foundational CS experience, but fewer than half feel equipped to teach it.

Global access to K–12 CS education has expanded, two-thirds of countries now offering or plan to offer it, doubling since 2019.

In the U.S., the number of graduates earning master's degrees in the field of AI nearly doubled between 2022 and 2023.

The United States is the global leader in producing ICT (information, technology, and communications) graduates, followed by Spain, Brazil, and the United Kingdom.

### Chapter 8: Public Opinion

Global optimism about AI has risen, with 55% of people seeing it as more beneficial than harmful as of 2024, up from 52% in 2022. In 18 of 26 nations surveyed by Ipsos, optimism increased.

Two-thirds of people globally believe AI-powered products and services will significantly impact daily life within the next three to five years. Two-thirds of people globally believe AI-powered products and services will significantly impact daily life within the next three to five years.

Regional differences in optimism remain stark. Majorities in China (83%), Indonesia (80%), and Thailand (77%) view AI as beneficial. In contrast, minorities in Canada (40%), United States (39%), and the Netherlands (36%) view it as beneficial.

In the U.S., 61% of people fear self-driving cars, and only 13% trust them (American Automobile Association survey).

Local U.S. policymakers support AI regulation. In 2023, 73.7% of the local policymakers agreed that AI should be regulated, { "//": "Increase from 55.7% in 2022" } 55.7% in 2022. In the U.S., support for regulation is stronger among Democrats (79.2%) versus Republicans (55.5%).

Local U.S. policymakers' specific priorities for regulation vary. There is strong support for stricter data privacy rules (80.4%), retraining for the unemployed (76.2%), and AI deployment regulations (72.5%). There is low support for a law enforcement facial recognition ban (34.2%), wage subsidies for wage declines (32.9%), and and universal basic income (24.6%).

Countries previously most skeptical of AI showed the sharpest gains in optimism from 2022 to 2024: Germany (+10%), France (+10%), Canada (+8%), Great Britain (+8%), and the United States (+4%).

Regarding the workforce, 60% of respondents agree AI will change how they do their jobs in the next five years, and 36% believe it will replace their jobs. People expect AI to save time (55%) and the offer better entertainment (51%), but are less confident in its impact on health (38%), the national economy (36%), and the job market (31%).
