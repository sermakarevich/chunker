# ai-index-2025-technical-performance-economics

**Parent:** [[index]]

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides a comprehensive analysis of AI model performance across coding, mathematics, reasoning, agentic capabilities, robotics, autonomous motion, and Responsible AI (RAI) frameworks. 

### AI Performance in Coding and Mathematics

In the domain of coding, the LMSYS Chatbot Arena for LLMs coding filter indicates that among Chinese models, DeepSeek-V3 leads with an Elo score of 1,317, trailing the highest-ranking model by 3.8%.

Mathematical problem-solving is evaluated using benchmarks that span from grade-school to competition-level mathematics. The GSM8K benchmark, introduced by OpenAI in 2021, contains approximately 8,000 diverse grade-school math word problems requiring multistep arithmetic solutions. The top-performing model on GSM8K is a variant of Claude Sonnet 3.5 optimized using the HPT prompting strategy, which achieved a score of 97.72%, a significant increase from the 2023 high of 91.00%. However, in 2024, several models from Mistral, Meta, and Qwen scored around 96%, suggesting that the GSM8K benchmark may be approaching saturation.

For more challenging tasks, the MATH dataset, introduced in 2021 by UC Berkeley and University of Chicago researchers, contains 12,500 competition-level mathematics problems. While AI systems initially solved only 6.9% of these problems, OpenAI’s o3-mini (high) model achieved a record score of 97.9% in January 2025, surpassing the human baseline of 90%.

Public preference in mathematics is tracked via the Chatbot Arena's math filter, which has collected over 340,000 public votes across 181 models. While Gemini-based models lead in the general and coding arenas, the top-ranked model in the Math Arena is an OpenAI o1 variant released in December 2024.

To address the saturation of high school and college-level benchmarks, Epoch AI introduced FrontierMath. This benchmark features hundreds of original, exceptionally challenging problems in areas such as number theory, real analysis, algebraic geometry, and category theory. Upon initial release, Gemini 1.5 Pro was the best-performing model but solved only 2.0% of the problems; other leading models, including o1-preview, o1-mini, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Grok 2 Beta, also showed very low success rates. However, OpenAI’s o3 model is reported to have scored 25.2% on FrontierMath.

In the area of theorem proving, Google DeepMind developed AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 for the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO). AlphaProof, a reinforcement learning system derived from AlphaZero, uses a fine-tuned Gemini model to translate natural language problems into the Lean formal proof system. AlphaGeometry 2 is a neuro-symbolic hybrid system using a Gemini-based language model trained on synthetic data. Together, they solved four out of six problems in the 2024 IMO: AlphaProof solved two algebra problems and one number theory problem, while AlphaGeometry 2 solved the sole geometry problem in 24 seconds. This performance level is equivalent to an IMO silver medalist, who typically solves 22.9 geometry problems in the IMO-AG-30 benchmarking set, whereas AlphaGeometry solved 25 out of 30 problems.

### AI Reasoning and General Intelligence

As older benchmarks like SQuAD and VQA have become saturated, researchers from the United States and Canada developed the Massive Multi-discipline Multimodal Understanding and Reasoning Benchmark (MMMU). MMMU consists of approximately 11,500 college-level questions across six core disciplines: art and design, business, science, health and medicine, humanities and social science, and technology and engineering. As of January 2025, OpenAI’s o1 is the highest-performing model on MMMU with a score of 78.2%, improving upon the state-of-the-art score of 59.4% reported in the 2024 AI Index, though it remains below the human expert baseline of 82.6%.

Regarding general, multisubject AI reasoning, researchers from NYU, Anthropic, and Meta introduced the GPQA benchmark to test AI systems. The GPQA dataset contains 448 difficult multiple-choice questions crafted by subject-matter experts in biology, physics, and chemistry, designed to be resistant to simple web searches. In the 2023 AI Index, the best-performing model, GPT-4, achieved a score of 38.8% on the GPQA diamond test set (the most challenging subset). By December 2024, OpenAI's o3 model achieved a state-of-the-art score of 87.7% on the diamond set, representing a 48.9 percentage point improvement over the 2023 state-of-the-art and becoming the first model to exceed the baseline accuracy of 81.3% set by expert human validators.

To evaluate the ability of AI systems to generalize beyond prior training, François Chollet introduced the ARC-AGI benchmark in 2019. ARC-AGI presents AI systems with independent tasks featuring demonstration or input pairs followed by test or output scenarios, requiring assumed prior knowledge (such as basic topology, objects, and elementary arithmetic) rather than specialized world knowledge. In 2020, the top-performing system on ARC-AGI scored 20%, and by 2024, the score rose to 33%. However, OpenAI's o3 model achieved a score of 75.7%, and when allocated a high compute budget exceeding the benchmark's $10,000 limit, it reached a score of 87.5%.

Due to the saturation of benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval, the team behind MMLU introduced Humanity's Last Exam (HLE), a benchmark comprising 2,700 highly challenging multimodal questions across dozens of subject areas. These questions were contributed by leading professors and graduate-level reviewers and were specifically tested against state-of-the-art LLMs before inclusion to ensure no existing model could answer them. Initial testing shows HLE is extremely difficult; OpenAI's o1 scores only 8.8%, while Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking (7.2%), Gemini 1.5 Pro (5.2%), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (4.8%), Grok-2 (3.9%), and GPT-4o (3.1%) score even lower. Researchers speculate that performance could exceed 50% by the end of 2025.

### Planning, Agentic Capabilities, and General Assistants

In the domain of planning—reasoning about actions that alter the world—Arizona State University proposed PlanBench, a suite of 600 problems based on the International Planning Competition. In the Blocksworld zero-shot evaluation, OpenAI's o1 scored 97.8%, significantly outperforming Llama 3.1 405B (62.6%) and GPT-4o (35.5%). In the more difficult Mystery Blocksworld domain, where answers are syntactically obfuscated, o1 scored 52.8%, while Llama 3.1 405B scored 0.8% and GPT-4 scored 0%.

Despite these gains, o1 solves only 23.6% of planning instances that require at least 20 steps. To test the R&D capabilities of AI systems, researchers launched RE-Bench in 2024, featuring seven open-ended machine learning research environments, such as optimizing a kernel or fine-tuning GPT-2. In short time-horizon settings (two-hour budget), the best AI systems score four times higher than human experts. In a 32-hour budget setting, however, humans outperform AI by a factor of two. In certain tasks, such as writing custom Triton kernels, AI agents can complete the work more quickly than any human expert.

Regarding general AI assistants, Meta introduced the GAIA benchmark in May 2024 to assess the ability of AI systems to perform a broad range of tasks, including reasoning, multimodal processing, web browsing, and tool use. GAIA consists of 466 questions that challenge models with complex, multistep problems requiring open web searches and reasoning through intricate scenarios. When GAIA was first launched, GPT-4 with plugins correctly answered only 15% of the the questions, while human respondents scored 92%. By 2024, the top system on GAIA reached a score of 65.1%, a roughly 30 percentage point increase over 2023.

AI agents—autonomous or semiautonomous systems designed to accomplish goals in specific environments—are harder to benchmark than image classification. In 2024, VisualAgentBench (VAB) was launched to test agents in three categories: embodied agents (household and gaming), GUI agents (mobile and web apps), and GraphQL design agents (CSS debugging). On the VAB test set, GPT-4o achieved the highest overall success rate at 36.2%, while most proprietary models averaged around 20%.

### Robotics and Autonomous Motion

In the field of robotics, the Robot Learning Benchmark (RLBench), launched in 2019, features 100 unique tasks. Evaluation typically focuses on a standardized subset of 18 tasks. As of January 2025, the top-performing model on this subset is SAM2Act—a collaboration between the University of Washington, Universidad Católica San Pablo, Nvidia, and the Allen Institute for AI—which achieved an 86.8% success rate, a 2.8 percentage point improvement over 2024 and a 66.7 percentage point increase from 2021.

Humanoid robotics saw significant growth in 2024. Figure AI launched Figure 02, a general-purpose humanoid robot standing 5 feet 6 inches tall, weighing 154 pounds, and capable of handling a 44-pound payload. Figure 02 can operate for up to five hours on a single charge and can perform tasks such as making coffee and assisting in automotive assembly by placing sheet metal into a car fixture. Integrated with OpenAI, Figure 02 can engage in speech-to-speech reasoning to explain its actions. Other notable humanoid robots include Tesla’s Optimus (launched in 2002 and redesigned in 2023) and Boston Dynamics’s Atlas.

Google DeepMind made significant strides in robotic advancements. In 2023, DeepMind launched PaLM-E and RT-2, utilizing transformer-based architectures and training on both manipulation and language data. In 2024, DeepMind introduced AutoRT, which uses foundation models to autonomously generate training data by coordinating multiple video-equipped robots, creating a dataset of 77,000 robotic trials across 6,650 unique tasks. DeepMind also developed SARA-RT, which reduces computational demands by converting the quadratic complexity of standard transformers into a linear model. Other contributions include the ALOHA (Autonomous and DemoStart Learning of High-level Activities) approach for intricate tasks like tying shoelaces and hanging a shirt, and DemoStart, an auto-curriculum reinforcement learning method. Furthermore, DeepMind introduced a robotic model in 2024 capable of reaching amateur human-level performance in competitive table tennis.

There was a significant push toward robotic foundation models in 2024. Nvidia introduced GR00T (Generalist Robot 00 Technology), a general-purpose foundation model for humanoid robots designed to mimic human movements and understand natural language, accompanied by data pipelines, simulation frameworks, and the Thor robotics computer. Other robotic foundation models include Covariant's RFM-1 and LLaRA (developed by Stony Brook University and the University of Wisconsin-Madison), which integrates perception, communication, and action into a single deep learning model.

Regarding self-driving cars, Waymo (an Alphabet subsidiary) operates robotaxis in Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Austin. As of January 2025, Waymo provides 150,000 paid rides per week, covering over a million miles. Through September 2024, Waymo recorded rider-only miles: 20.823 million in Phoenix, 10.209 million in San Francisco, 1.947 million in Los Angeles, and 124,000 in Austin. Waymo plans to test vehicles in 10 additional cities, including Las Vegas, San Diego, and Miami, and has tested in snowy conditions in upstate New York and Truckee, California.

In China, Baidu’s Apollo Go reported 988,000 rides in Q3 2024, a 20% year-over-year increase. As of October 2024, Apollo Go operated 400 robotaxis with plans to expand to 1,000 by the end of 2025. In Europe, startups like Wayve are gaining traction. Pony.AI plans to scale its fleet from 200 to between 2,000 and 3,000 vehicles by the end of 2026. Baidu's Apollo Go launched the RT6 robotaxi, featuring a battery-swapping system and priced at $30,000.

Tesla unveiled the Cybercab in October 2024, a two-passenger autonomous vehicle without a steering wheel or pedals, expected in 2026 at a price under $30,000. Tesla also unveil the Robovan, an electric autonomous van for 20 passengers. Uber has partnered with WeRide to develop an autonomous ride-sharing platform in Abu Dhabi.

In autonomous freight, Kodiak completed first driverless deliveries, and Aurora has reported over 1 million miles of autonomous freight hauling on U.S. highways since 2021 (with human safety drivers), delaying its commercial launch to April 2025.

To evaluate autonomous motion, new benchmarks were introduced in 2024. NuPlan (by Motional) features 1,282 hours of diverse driving scenarios and a training set of over 2 million fully annotated frames. Bench2Drive provides a closed-loop testing simulation environment where top performers include TCP* (64.22), DriveAdapter (49.30), and ThinkTwice (40.73). OpenAD is the first real-world, open-world autonomous driving benchmark for 3D object detection.

Safety data indicates self-driving cars may be safer than human drivers. Waymo data for Phoenix and San Francisco shows significantly fewer incidents: 0.32 airbag deployments compared to 5.91 for humans, 0.90 injury-reported crashes compared to 40.06 for humans, and 1.74 police-reported incidents compared to 2.26 for humans. The percentage difference to the human benchmark is -88% for airbag deployments in Phoenix and -87% in San Francisco, -87% for injury-reported crashes in Phoenix and San Francisco, and -77% for police-reported incidents in Phoenix and -88% in San Francisco. A Waymo and Swiss Re study of 25.3 million miles found an 88% reduction in property damage claims and a 92% reduction in bodily injury claims compared to human baselines; Waymo had only nine property damage claims and two bodily injury claims, while humans would be expected to have 78 and 26, respectively.

### Responsible AI (RAI) Frameworks and Governance

Chapter 3 of the report establishes a comprehensive framework defining key dimensions: Privacy (the right to confidentiality and anonymity), Data Governance (policies for quality, access, and licensing), Fairness and Bias (algorithms avoiding discrimination), Transparency (Transparency of system mechanics), Explainability (the capacity to articulate the rationale behind outputs), and Security and Safety (integrity against threats and minimization of harm).

In a 2024 partnership between the AI Index and McKinsey & Company, a survey of 759 business leaders from over 30 countries found that AI governance is not dominated by a single department; information security (cyber/fraud/privacy) is the most common department with primary oversight at 21%, followed by data and analytics at 17%, and 14% of respondents reported dedicated AI governance roles.

Privacy and Data Governance specifically focus on the right to confidentiality, anonymity, and the right to correct misrepresented sensitive information. This necessitates compliance with legal frameworks like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). A large-scale audit of over 1,800 text datasets used for training foundation models revealed that more than 70% of datasets on popular hosting sites lacked adequate license information, and 50% of the licenses were miscategorized. An audit of dataset licenses compared against GitHub, GitHub, Papers with Code, and Hugging Face showed sharp differences in classifications (commercial, unspecified, noncommercial, and academic-only), highlighting a lack of clarity in licensing.

To mitigate 'data contamination' (where training data includes test set examples), the report emphasizes the use of 'canary' strings—unique, identifiable sequences of text—to detect leaks and improve data provenance. To prevent the risk of 'jailbreaking' or 'prompt injection' that can bypass safety filters, the report emphasizes the importance of RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) and red-teaming as methods to align model outputs with safety guidelines and mitigate these risks.

There is also a growing tension between massive datasets and data control, as evidenced by the increase of 'AI.txt' files and the use of robots.txt to block AI crawlers.

### Bias, Ethical Incidents, and Factuality

AI model bias remains a pervasive issue, resulting in 'representational harm' (stereotypical or harmful depictions of certain groups) and 'algorithmic bias' (algorithmic bias: poor performance for marginalized groups). The report notes that models often associate negative terms with Black individuals, favor men for leadership roles, and associate women with humanities rather than STEM fields.

To track ethical misuses, the AI Incident Database (AIID) recorded a record high of 233 incidents in 2024, a 56.4% increase over 2023. Specific incidents include:
- Deepfake harassment: In June 2024, 15-year-old Elliston Berry from Texas was targeted by a classmate using a clothes-removal app to execute fake nude images via photos from her private Instagram account.
- Facial recognition errors: In May 2024, a woman in the U.K. was mistakenly identified as a shoplifter by the Facewatch system at a Home Bargains store.
- Identity exploitation: In October 2024, a lawsuit against Character.AI was filed after a chatbot used the name and image of a deceased murder victim from 2006, Jennifer Ann Crecente.
- Harmful AI companionship: In October 2024, 14-year-old Sewell Setzer III died by suicide after interacting with a chatbot character named "Dany" on Character.AI, who allegedly provided harmful advice.

Academic interest in RAI has risen, with RAI papers accepted at leading conferences increasing by 28.8% from 992 in 2023 to 1,278 in 2024.

To address hallucination and factuality, several benchmarks are used in 2024. The Hughes Hallucination Evaluation Model (HHEM) evaluates hallucination rates in summaries of the CNN and Daily Mail corpus. GLM-4-9b-Chat and Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp are tied for the lowest hallucination rate at 1.3%, and followed by o1-mini (1.4%) and GPT-4o (1.5%).

Google's FACTS Grounding benchmark assesses how LLMs form responses based on context documents across finance, technology, retail, medicine, and law. Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp holds the highest grounding score of 83.6%.

OpenAI's SimpleQA evaluates long AI answers with multiple factual claims across history, science and technology, art, and geography. OpenAI's o1-preview scored 42.7%, while Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 44.5% for "correct-given-attempted" prompts. Some families, such as Claude-3, refrained from responding to 75% of the prompts.

There is a lack of standardized RAI benchmarks. While general capability benchmarks like MMLU, GPQA Diamond, and MATH are used, RAI evaluations are often internal and proprietary. Some models are tested against BBQ, HarmBench, Cybench, SimpleQA, Toxic WildChat, StrongREJECT, the WMDP benchmark, and MakeMePay or MakeMeSay. For example, Llama 3.3 was tested against BBQ, and o1 was tested against SimpleQA and the WMDP benchmark.

### Global Policy and Strategic Implementation

Organizations are currently balancing innovation and safety. On a global scale, there are shifts toward mandatory safety audits and transparency reports for high-risk AI systems. Two major initiatives launched in early 2025: the International Network of AI Safety Institutes (established February 2025), uniting the EU and nine countries to advance AI safety and provide technical solutions. The Arab League launched the Arab Dialogue Circle on “Artificial Intelligence in the Arab World: Innovative Applications and Ethical Challenges• to focus on AI innovations and ethics.

Beyond technical governance, the report highlights the use of AI to influence political and social discourse, specifically regarding the 2024 election cycle. A primary concern is the use of AI to create deceptive content, such as fake images or audio to mislead voters, and the use of AI-generated 'deepfakes' to impersonate political figures. Furthermore, AI can be used to automate the creation of disinformation campaigns, allowing for the rapid spread of false narratives at scale, risking the undermining of democratic processes by eroding trust in information and manipulating public opinion.

### Economic and Labor Market Dimensions

Beyond technical performance, the 2025 AI Index Report analyzes the economic and labor dimensions of the AI landscape in 2024. Organizations are moving from experimental use of AI to strategic integration, driving a notable increase in AI-related investments and growth as companies seek efficiency and innovation through AI-enabled operations.

Labor market trends show a surge in demand for AI-competent professionals. This demand extends beyond technical roles like software engineering and data science into professional functions in healthcare, finance, and manufacturing. There is a a strong correlation between the adoption of AI tools and the demand for employees with the skills to operate them. 

In terms of talent distribution, the United States leads in the relative penetration of AI skills. While North America and Asia have the highest absolute number of skilled professionals, growth in AI talent is most rapid in emerging markets. However, a persistent gender gap exists; in the US, the penetration of AI skills is notably higher among men than women. Additionally, individuals with advanced degrees in STEM fields are disproportionately represented in the AI talent pool.

The labor market is navigating a tension between job displacement and automation and the emergence of new specialized roles. New roles include AI auditors, prompt engineers, and AI ethics officers. To manage this transition, there is a growing emphasis on 'upskilling' and 'reskilling' the workforce, which is mirrored by educational institutions incorporating AI literacy into their curricula to prepare graduates for an AI-augmented job market.

In summary, the Technical Performance and Economic dimensions of the AI Index Report 2025 analyze the trajectory of AI from record-breaking performance in benchmarks like GPQA Diamond (OpenAI o3: 87.7%) and ARC-AGI (OpenAI o3: 87.5% with high compute), to the expansion of humanoid robots like Figure 02 and the deployment of robotaxis like Waymo's 150,000 weekly paid rides, alongside a growing focus on global governance of Responsible AI and the shift toward strategic AI integration in the business economy.
