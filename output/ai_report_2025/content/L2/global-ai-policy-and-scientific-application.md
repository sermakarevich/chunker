# global-ai-policy-and-scientific-application

**Parent:** [[index]]

The global landscape of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is currently defined by a complex interplay between strategic scientific application, national security, and the development of formal regulatory frameworks across major global powers. This evolution is marked by a shift toward formal regulation and the strategic integration of AI into specialized scientific domains to accelerate discovery and maintain global competitiveness.

### Global Regulatory Frameworks

Regulatory approaches to AI vary significantly by jurisdiction, reflecting different political and legal priorities. In the United States, the regulatory environment is characterized by a distinct contrast between federal and state-level activity. Between 2014 and 2024, the U.S. Congress introduced a total of 133 bills related to AI, while in 2023 alone, 35 U.S. states introduced AI-related bills. The U.S. focus has been heavily weighted toward AI safety, regulation, and the specific impacts of AI on labor and copyright laws. At the federal level, the U.S. government has integrated AI into operational sectors including healthcare and defense, establishing frameworks for its use within federal agencies. This is supported by a substantial increase in public funding for AI, distributed across basic research and applied applications, with the U.S. government acting as a central coordinator and financier of AI progress.

In the European Union, the EU AI Act serves as the primary regulatory pillar. This act adopts a risk-based approach to ensure safety and the protection of fundamental rights. The framework categorizes AI systems into four distinct risk levels: unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal. Systems designated as 'high-risk' are subject to strict obligations, requiring them to meet rigorous safety and transparency standards before they can be deployed.

China's regulatory efforts have taken a more application-specific approach. Rather than implementing a broad horizontal framework, Chinese regulation focuses on particular technologies such as recommendation algorithms and 'deep synthesis.' Providers of these technologies are required to ensure that the generated content is truthful and that users are explicitly notified when they are interacting with an AI system.

Other global regions, including Canada, are similarly engaged in balancing the pursuit of technological innovation with the necessity of implementing safeguards to mitigate associated risks. This tension between fostering innovation and ensuring safety remains a central theme in global AI governance throughout 2024.

### AI-Generated Content and the Management of Deepfakes

In the United States, legislative efforts are heavily focused on the creation and dissemination of deceptive media, specifically AI-generated content and deepfakes. Both state-level regulations and federal proposals are being implemented to reach three primary objectives: enforcing transparency through the explicit labeling of AI-generated content to prevent deception, protecting individuals' likenesses from unauthorized AI synthesis (preventing the cloning of a person's image or voice without permission), and penalizing the use of deepfakes in electoral contexts to prevent voter manipulation and protect the integrity of democratic processes from the influence of synthetic media.

### AI in Scientific Discovery and Specialized Applications

Parallel to legislative developments, AI is being increasingly applied to accelerate scientific discovery across multiple domains. In materials science and drug discovery, AI is utilized to predict molecular properties and optimize designs, which significantly reduces the time required for traditional laboratory experimentation. AI's role has also expanded into climate modeling, where it is used to analyze complex atmospheric patterns and improve the accuracy of environmental predictions.

Furthermore, the development of autonomous systems remains a priority, though this is coupled with an intensifying focus on AI safety and alignment. This alignment work is critical to ensure that as AI systems become more autonomous and capable, they remain beneficial to humanity and operate within intended ethical and safety boundaries.

### US Domestic Policy and Social Integration

Within the United States, there is a a multifaceted approach to AI integration that seeks to balance global competitiveness with ethics and individual rights. This involves a dual-track approach: combining heavy public investment in basic and applied research with targeted legislation against deepfakes and a broader movement toward ethical AI regulation. 

One area of significant social integration is education. There is a concerted effort to integrate computing into school curricula, though the degree of this integration is inconsistent, varying widely across different regions and educational institutions. 

Ultimately, the strategic policy of the U.S. is to create a regulatory environment that encourages the development of a high-performance AI ecosystem while mitigating the risks of harm, thereby maintaining the United States' terms of global competitiveness in the rapidly evolving AI landscape.
