# ai-math-reasoning-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-2025-technical-performance|ai-index-2025-technical-performance]] — The AI Index Report 2025 analyzes technical performance across coding, math, and reasoning, highlighting records such as OpenAI's o3 achieving 97.9% on the MATH dataset and 87.7% on the GPQA diamond set, while noting the saturation of benchmarks like GSM8K.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides data on AI model performance across coding, mathematics, and reasoning. In the domain of coding, the LMSYS Chatbot Arena for LLMs coding filter shows that among Chinese models, DeepSeek-V3 leads with an Elo score of 1,317, which trails the highest-ranking model by 3.8%.

Regarding mathematical problem-solving, benchmarks evaluate an AI system's ability to reason mathematically across levels ranging from grade-school to competition-standard mathematics. The GSM8K benchmark, introduced by OpenAI in 2021, consists of approximately 8,000 diverse grade-school math word problems requiring multistep arithmetic solutions. The top-performing model on GSM8K is a variant of Claude Sonnet 3.5 optimized using the HPT prompting strategy, which achieved a score of 97.72%, a significant increase from the 2023 high of 91.00%. However, in 2024, several models from Mistral, Meta, and Qwen scored around 96%, suggesting the GSM8K benchmark may be approaching saturation.

For more challenging tasks, the MATH dataset, introduced in 2021 by UC Berkeley and University of Chicago researchers, contains 12,500 competition-level mathematics problems. While AI systems initially solved only 6.9% of these problems, OpenAI’s o3-mini (high) model achieved a record score of 97.9% in January 2025, surpassing the human baseline of 90%.

Public preference in mathematics is tracked via the Chatbot Arena's math filter, which has collected over 340,000 public votes across 181 models. Unlike the general and coding arenas where Gemini-based models lead, the top-ranked model in the Math Arena is an OpenAI o1 variant released in December 2024.

To address the saturation of high school and college-level benchmarks, Epoch AI introduced FrontierMath, a benchmark featuring hundreds of original, exceptionally challenging problems in areas such as number theory, real analysis, algebraic geometry, and category theory. When first released, the best-performing model, Gemini 1.5 Pro, solved only 2.0% of the problems, while other leading models including o1-preview, o1-mini, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Grok 2 Beta also showed very low success rates. However, OpenAI’s o3 model is reported to have scored 25.2% on FrontierMath.

In the area of theorem proving, Google DeepMind developed AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 for the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO). AlphaProof, a reinforcement learning system derived from AlphaZero, uses a fine-tuned Gemini model to translate natural language problems into the Lean formal proof system. AlphaGeometry 2 is a neuro-symbolic hybrid system using a Gemini-based language model trained on synthetic data. Together, they solved four out of six problems in the 2024 IMO: AlphaProof solved two algebra problems and one number theory problem, while AlphaGeometry 2 solved the sole geometry problem in 24 seconds. This achieved a performance level equivalent to an IMO silver medalist, who typically solves 22.9 geometry problems in the IMO-AG-30 benchmarking set, whereas AlphaGeometry solved 25 out of 30 problems.

Finally, the report discusses general reasoning. As older benchmarks like SQuAD and VQA have become saturated, researchers from the United States and Canada developed the Massive Multi-discipline Multimodal Understanding and Reasoning Benchmark (MMMU). MMMU contains approximately 11,500 college-level questions across six core disciplines: art and design, business, science, health and medicine, humanities and social science, and technology and engineering. As of January 2025, OpenAI’s o1 is the highest-performing model on MMMU with a score of 78.2%, improving upon the state-of-the-art score of 59.4% reported in the 2024 AI Index, though it remains below the human expert baseline of 82.6%.
