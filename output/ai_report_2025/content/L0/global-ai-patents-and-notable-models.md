# global-ai-patents-and-notable-models

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-research-patents-models-trends|ai-research-patents-models-trends]] — The AI Index Report 2025 reveals a China-led surge in AI research publications (23.2% share) and patents (69.7% share) by 2023, while the U.S. leads in notable AI models (40 in 2024) and top-cited research. The report also highlights a training compute doubling every five months and a massive reduction in inference costs, such as GPT-3.5 level performance costs falling from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens.

The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, includes a section on global AI patents and notable AI models. 

Regarding AI patents, the report utilizes patent-level bibliographic records from PATSTAT Global, a comprehensive database provided by the European Patent Office (EPO). Due to data availability delays, 2023 is the most recent year with accessible data. From 2010 to 2023, the number of granted AI patents worldwide grew steadily from 3,833 to 122,511, with a 29.6% increase in the last year (2023).

In terms of regional distribution, 82.4% of the world's granted AI patents as of 2023 originated from East Asia and the Pacific, followed by North America at 14.2%. The gap in AI patent grants between East Asia and the Pacific and North America has widened steadily since 2010. Disaggregated by geographic area, China led with 69.7% of granted AI patents in 2023, followed by the United States at 14.2%. The share of AI patents from the United States declined from a peak of 42.8% in 2015. 

When analyzing AI patents per 100,000 inhabitants in 2023, South Korea had the most with 17.27, followed by Luxembourg (15.31), China (6.08), the United States (5.20), and Japan (4.58). Between 2013 and 2023, Luxembourg (8,216%), China (6,317%), and Sweden (3,453%) experienced the greatest percentage increase in AI patenting per capita.

Additionally, the report examines "notable machine learning models," a term defined by the data provider Epoch AI to designate influential models selected based on state-of-the-art advancements, historical significance, or high citation rates. In 2024, the United States led in the production of notable AI models with 40 models, followed by China with 15 and France with three. All major geographic groups, including the United States, China, and Europe, released fewer notable models in 2024 than in 2023. Since 2003, the United States has produced more notable models than other major countries such as the United Kingdom, China, and Canada.

By sector, industry has led in releasing notable machine learning models since 2014. In 2024, industry produced 55 notable AI models, and Epoch AI identified no notable AI models originating from academia in 2024. The proportion of notable AI models originating from industry grew to 90.2% in 2024. By organization, Google led the production of notable models over the past decade with 187 models, followed by Meta (82) and Microsoft (39). In 2024, the top contributors were Google (7 models), OpenAI (7 models), and Alibaba (6 models). Among academic institutions since 2014, Carnegie Mellon University (25), Stanford University (25), and Tsinghua University (22) have been the most prolific.
