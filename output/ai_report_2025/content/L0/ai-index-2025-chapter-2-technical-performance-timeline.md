# ai-index-2025-chapter-2-technical-performance-timeline

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-2025-report-synthesis-and-technical-timeline|ai-index-2025-report-synthesis-and-technical-timeline]] — The AI Index Report 2025 documents 2024's trends including U.S. AI investment of $109.1 billion, SWE-bench scores rising 67.3 percentage points, inference costs dropping 280-fold, and a chronological timeline of 32 model releases featuring GPT-4o, Llama 3.1 405B, and o3, with ARC-AGI records reaching 87.5% and open-weight models closing the gap to closed models from 8% to 1.7%.

The following section details Chapter 2: Technical Performance, Section 2.1: Overview of AI in 2024, from the AI Index Report. The AI Index Steering Committee selected the most notable model and dataset releases of 2024. The chronological timeline below lists each release's date, name, category, creator, and significance, along with accompanying figure source attributions:

- January 19, 2024: Stable LM 2 (LLM, Stability AI). Stability's latest language model builds on the original Stable LM with enhanced performance. At 1.6 billion parameters, it is designed to run efficiently on portable devices like laptops and smartphones. (Figure 2.1.1, Source: Wikipedia, 2025)
- February 8, 2024: Aya Dataset (Dataset, Cohere for AI/Beijing Academy of AI/Cohere/Binghamton University). A collection of 513 million prompt-completion pairs spanning 114 languages, released as part of Cohere's Aya initiative. The accompanying paper and dataset represent significant milestones in multilingual instruction tuning. (Figure 2.1.2, Source: Cohere, 2025)
- February 15, 2024: Gemini 1.5 Pro (LLM, Google DeepMind). Google's Gemini model set a new benchmark with a 1M token context window, far exceeding GPT-4 Turbo's 128K token limit. (Figure 2.1.3, Source: Google, 2024)
- February 20, 2024: SDXL-Lightning (Text-to-image, ByteDance). Developed by TikTok's creators, this model was among the fastest text-to-image systems at release, generating high-quality synthetic images in under a second using progressive adversarial distillation instead of diffusion-based techniques. (Figure 2.1.4, Source: Hugging Face, 2025)
- February 20, 2024: Claude 3 (LLM, Anthropic). Anthropic's latest LLM outperforms GPT-4 and Gemini on nearly all industry benchmarks, reduces incorrect prompt refusals, and delivers significantly higher accuracy. (Figure 2.1.5, Source: Anthropic, 2025)
- March 7, 2024: Inflection-2.5 (LLM, Inflection AI). Inflection's flagship product "Pi" featured an exceptional model with GPT-4–level performance while using only 40% of its computing resources. Microsoft acquired Inflection for $650 million just two weeks after release. (Figure 2.1.6, Source: Inflection, 2025)
- March 19, 2024: Moirai and LOTSA (Model/dataset, Salesforce). Salesforce unveiled Moirai, a foundation model for universal forecasting, alongside LOTSA, a diverse, large-scale time series dataset with 27 billion observations spanning nine domains. (Figure 2.1.7, Source: Salesforce, 2025)
- March 27, 2024: DBRX (LLM, Databricks). Databricks' open-source mixture-of-experts (MoE) LLM is a fine-grained model that surpasses similar small MoE models like Mixtral and Grok. This transformer decoder-only model features 132B parameters (36B active per input) and was trained on 12 trillion tokens. (Figure 2.1.8, Source: Databricks, 2025)
- April 2, 2024: Stable Audio 2 (Text-to-song and song-to-song, Stability AI). The latest version of Stable Audio now supports audio-to-audio functionality, allowing users to upload songs and manipulate them using natural language prompts. (Figure 2.1.9, Source: Stability AI, 2025)
- April 17, 2024: Llama 3 (LLM, Meta). The Llama 3 series debuts with 8B and 70B parameter text-based models, ranking among the highest performing models of their size to date. (Figure 2.1.10, Source: Meta, 2025)
- May 13, 2024: GPT-4o (Multimodal, OpenAI). A new multimodal model capable of processing inputs in any combination of text, audio, images, and video, and generating outputs in the same formats. It responds to audio in as little as 320 milliseconds, matching human response times. (Figure 2.1.11, Source: OpenAI, 2024)
- June 7, 2024: Qwen2 (LLM, Alibaba). Developed by China's Alibaba, Qwen2 is a series of advanced base and instruction-tuned models that rival competitors like Llama 3-70B and Mixtral-8x22B in performance across numerous benchmarks. (Figure 2.1.12, Source: Qwen, 2024)
- June 17, 2024: Runway Gen-3 (Text-to-video and image-to-video, Runway). Runway's upgraded video generation model excels at creating photorealistic humans with vivid and expressive emotionality. (Figure 2.1.13, Source: Runway, 2024)
- July 23, 2024: Llama 3.1 405B (LLM, Meta). Meta's largest model to date and final entry in the Llama 3.1 family, featuring 405B parameters. It became the most capable openly available foundation model upon release, rivaling many closed models across benchmarks. (Figure 2.1.14, Source: Meta, 2024)
- August 12, 2024: Falcon Mamba (LLM, Technology Innovation Institute in Abu Dhabi). A powerful 7B parameter model built on the Mamba State Space Language Model (SSLM) architecture. It enables Falcon to dynamically adjust parameters and filter out irrelevant inputs, making it more efficient than transformer-based models. (Figure 2.1.15, Source: Hugging Face, 2025)
- August 13, 2024: Grok-2 (Text-to-text and text-to-image, xAI). xAI's advanced text- and image-generation model excels in image creation, advanced reasoning, and problem-solving. It rivaled leading models quickly despite xAI being founded only in March 2023. (Figure 2.1.16, Source: xAI, 2025)
- August 15, 2024: Imagen 3 (Text-to-image, Google Labs). Google's updated AI image generator achieves the highest Elo score on the GenAI-Bench image benchmark, setting a new standard for AI-generated visuals. (Figure 2.1.17, Source: Google, 2025)
- August 22, 2024: Jamba 1.5 (LLM, AI21 Labs). The first LLM to combine state-space models with transformers, delivering high-quality text-based results. This hybrid approach significantly enhances speed while preserving output quality. (Figure 2.1.18, Source: AI21, 2025)
- August 29, 2024: SynthID v2 (Tool, Google). The updated version of Google's watermarking and identification software now supports AI-generated content across images, video, audio, and text, with enhanced tracking and verification capabilities. (Figure 2.1.19, Source: Google, 2025)
- September 11, 2024: NotebookLM Podcast Tool (Text-to-podcast, Google Labs). The second end-to-end AI podcast generator to hit the market after Synthpod, it went viral among students for studying and tech employees for listening to AI-generated summaries. (Figure 2.1.20, Source: Google, 2025)
- September 12, 2024: o1-preview (Language, math, biology, OpenAI). OpenAI's first "o series" model designed for advanced reasoning and complex tasks. It is significantly more powerful than GPT, particularly in math, science, and coding. (Figure 2.1.21, Source: OpenAI, 2025)
- September 17, 2024: NVLM (D, H, X) (Vision/language, Nvidia). Nvidia's three open-access models for vision-language tasks achieved top scores on OCRBench (optical character recognition) and VQAv2 (natural language understanding). (Figure 2.1.22, Source: Dai et al., 2024)
- September 19, 2024: Qwen2.5 (LLM, Alibaba). Alibaba's latest foundation model series includes efficient smaller models and specialized coding and math models for targeted functionality. (Figure 2.1.23, Source: Qwen, 2025)
- October 16, 2024: Ministral (LLM, Mistral). A pair of compact models (3B and 8B parameters) that outperformed Gemma and Llama models of similar size across all major industry-recognized benchmarks. (Figure 2.1.24, Source: Mistral, 2025)
- October 22, 2024: Anthropic Computer Use (Agentic Capability, Anthropic). A groundbreaking computer control feature for Claude 3.5 Sonnet users that allows the model to move the cursor, type, and autonomously complete tasks on the user's computer in real time. (Figure 2.1.25, Source: Anthropic, 2025)
- October 28, 2024: Apple Intelligence (iPhone feature, Apple). A suite of AI-powered features including Image Playground, Genmoji, and Siri integration with ChatGPT. (Figure 2.1.26, Source: Apple, 2025)
- December 3, 2024: Nova Pro (Multimodal, Amazon). Amazon Web Services' most powerful model in the Nova family, capable of processing visual and textual information, with particular strength in analyzing financial documents. (Figure 2.1.27, Source: Amazon, 2025)
- December 11, 2024: Gemini 2 (LLM, Google DeepMind). The improved Gemini version includes computer control alongside image and audio generation capabilities. It is twice as fast as Gemini 1.5 Pro and offers significantly enhanced performance in coding and image analysis. (Figure 2.1.28, Source: Google, 2025)
- December 12, 2024: Sora (Text-to-video, OpenAI). A video generation model that creates videos up to 20 seconds long at 1080p for ChatGPT Pro users and five seconds at 720p for ChatGPT Plus users. Official release was delayed from early 2024 demos to improve model safety. (Figure 2.1.29, Source: OpenAI, 2025)
- December 13, 2024: Global MMLU (Dataset, Cohere). A multilingual evaluation set featuring professionally translated MMLU questions across 42 languages. It evaluates AI performance in diverse languages while addressing Western biases in the original MMLU dataset, where an estimated 28% of questions rely on Western cultural knowledge. (Figure 2.1.30, Source: Singh et al., 2025)
- December 20, 2024: o3 (beta) (Multimodal, OpenAI). OpenAI's newest frontier model, released for safety testing by AI researchers. It outperforms all previous models in SWE, competition code, competition math, PhD-level science, and research math benchmarks, and sets a new record on the ARC-AGI benchmark with 87.5% on the ARC Prize team's private holdout set. (Figure 2.1.31, Source: VentureBeat, 2025)
- December 27, 2024: DeepSeek-V3 (LLM, DeepSeek). An open-source model developed with significantly fewer computing resources than state-of-the-art models that outperforms leading models on benchmarks like MMLU and GPQA. (Figure 2.1.32, Source: Dirox, 2025)

State of AI Performance
The AI Index provides a high-level view into major AI trends that occurred in 2024. Figure 2.1.33 illustrates the progress of AI systems relative to human baselines for eight AI benchmarks corresponding to eleven tasks (such as image classification or basic-level reading comprehension). The AI Index team selected one benchmark to represent each task. This year, the AI Index team added newly released benchmarks, GPQA Diamond and MMMU, to showcase the progress of AI systems in tackling extremely challenging cognitive tasks. The chart tracks performance relative to the human baseline (%) from 2012 to 2024 across the following benchmarks: Image classification (ImageNet Top-5), Visual reasoning (VQA), Medium-level reading comprehension (SQuAD 2.0), English language understanding (SuperGLUE), Multitask language understanding (MMLU), Competition-level mathematics (MATH), PhD-level science questions (GPQA Diamond), and Multimodal understanding and reasoning (MMMU). As of 2024, there are very few task categories where human performance remains unmatched.

Note 1: An AI benchmark is a standardized test used to evaluate the performance and capabilities of AI systems on specific tasks. For example, ImageNet is a canonical AI benchmark that features a large collection of labeled images, and AI systems are tasked with classifying these images accurately. Tracking progress on benchmarks has been a standard way for the AI community to monitor the advancement of AI systems.
Note 2: In Figure 2.1.33, the values are scaled to establish a standard metric for comparing different benchmarks. The scaling function is calibrated such that the performance of the best model for each year is measured as a percentage of the human baseline for a given task. A value of 105% indicates, for example, that a model performs 5% better than the human baseline.
