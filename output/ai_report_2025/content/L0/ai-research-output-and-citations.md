# ai-research-output-and-citations

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-research-patents-models-trends|ai-research-patents-models-trends]] — The AI Index Report 2025 reveals a China-led surge in AI research publications (23.2% share) and patents (69.7% share) by 2023, while the U.S. leads in notable AI models (40 in 2024) and top-cited research. The report also highlights a training compute doubling every five months and a massive reduction in inference costs, such as GPT-3.5 level performance costs falling from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens.

According to the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, AI research output and influence are distributed geographically, by sector, and by topic. In 2023, East Asia and the Pacific led in AI research output, accounting for 34.5% of all AI publications, followed by Europe and Central Asia at 18.2% and North America at 10.3%. In terms of influence, East Asia and the Pacific accounted for the largest share of AI publication citations in 2023 at 37.1%. While the citation shares of East Asia and the Pacific and North America were roughly equal in 2017, North American and European citation shares have since declined while East Asia and the Pacific's share has risen sharply.

On a country level, China was the global leader in AI article publications in 2023, accounting for 23.2% of the total, compared to 15.2% from Europe and 9.2% from India and the United States (each at 9.2%). Since 2016, China's share of publications has steadily increased, while the proportion attributed to the United States remained relatively stable until 2021 before showing a slight decline. In 2023, Chinese AI publications accounted for 22.6% of all AI citations, followed by Europe at 20.9% and the United States at 13.0%. The late 2010s marked the turning point when China surpassed Europe and the U.S. as the leading source of AI publication citations.

By sector, academic institutions remained the primary source of AI publications worldwide in 2023, accounting for 84.9% of all publications, compared to 85.9% in 2013. Industry contributed 7.1% of AI publications in 2023, followed by government institutions at 4.9% and nonprofit organizations at 1.7%. The proportion of AI publications from industry varies by region: in the United States, 16.5% of AI publications come from industry, whereas in China, this figure is 8.0%. Conversely, China has the highest percentage of AI publications originating from the education sector at 84.5%.

Regarding research topics, machine learning was the most prevalent topic in AI publications in 2023, comprising 75.7% of publications, followed by computer vision (47.2%), pattern recognition (25.9%), and natural language processing (17.1%). There has also been a sharp increase in publications on generative AI.

Focusing on the 100 most-cited AI publications from 2021 to 2023, the United States consistently had the highest number of top-cited publications, with 64 in 2021, 59 in 2022, and 50 in 2023, though its share has gradually declined. China ranked second in each of these years. By sector, academia consistently produced the most top-cited AI publications, with 34 in 2021, 27 in 2022, and 42 in 2023. In contrast, industry contributions dropped from 17 in 2021 and 19 in 2022 to 7 in 2023. By organization, Google led each year from 2021 to 2023, tying with Tsinghua University in 2023 when both contributed eight publications to the top 100. In 2023, Carnegie Mellon University was the highest-ranked U.S. academic institution.
