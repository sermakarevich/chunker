# autonomous-vehicles-performance-safety

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-robotics-autonomous-motion-performance|ai-robotics-autonomous-motion-performance]] — The AI Index Report 2025 details advancements in AI assistants (GAIA benchmark scores rising to 65.1%), humanoid robotics (Figure 02 and GR00T), and autonomous vehicles, noting that Waymo vehicles show an 88% reduction in property damage claims compared to human drivers over 25.3 million miles.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, details advancements in autonomous motion and robotics. 

Regarding self-driving vehicles, Tesla unveiled the Cybercab in October 2024, a two-passenger autonomous vehicle without a steering wheel or pedals, expected to enter production in 2026 at a price under $30,000. Tesla also unveiled the Robovan, an electric autonomous van capable of transporting up to 20 passengers. In China, Baidu's Apollo Go launched the RT6 robotaxi, which features a battery-swapping system and a price tag of $30,000. Business partnerships have also expanded, such as Uber's collaboration with WeRide—the first publicly listed robotaxi company—to develop an autonomous ride-sharing platform in Abu Dhabi. 

In the realm of autonomous freight, companies like Kodiak have completed their first driverless deliveries. Aurora has reported over 1 million miles of autonomous freight hauling on U.S. highways since 2021, although these miles were completed with human safety drivers present. Aurora has also delayed the commercial launch of its fleet from the end of 2024 to April 2025.

To evaluate these capabilities, new benchmarks were introduced in 2024. NuPlan, developed by Motional, is a large-scale autonomous driving dataset and evaluation framework for testing machine-learning-based motion planners, featuring 1,282 hours of diverse driving scenarios and a training set of over 2 million fully annotated frames from more than 10,000 clips. Bench2Drive provides a comprehensive, realistic, closed-loop testing simulation environment for end-to-end autonomous vehicles to address the limitations of open-loop evaluations, which often lead to models that memorize patterns rather than learning to drive. According to the Bench2Drive benchmark, various autonomous driving methods achieved different driving scores, with top performers including TCP* (64.22), DriveAdapter (49.30), and ThinkTwice (40.73). Additionally, OpenAD is the first real-world, open-world autonomous driving benchmark for 3D object detection, focusing on domain generalization (the ability to adapt across diverse sensor configurations) and open-vocabulary recognition (the ability to identify previously unseen semantic categories).

Safety data suggests that self-driving cars may be safer than human drivers. Data for Waymo vehicles in Phoenix and San Francisco shows they have significantly fewer incidents per million miles than human benchmarks: 0.32 airbag deployments compared to 5.91 for humans, 0.90 injury-reported crashes compared to 4.06 for humans, and 1.74 police-reported incidents compared to 2.26 for humans. Waymo's performance shows a percentage difference to the human benchmark of -88% for airbag deployments in Phoenix and -87% in San Francisco, -78% for injury-reported crashes in Phoenix and -76% in San Francisco, and -77% for police-reported incidents in Phoenix and -88% in San Francisco.

Furthermore, a study conducted by Waymo and Swiss Re, a leading reinsurer, analyzed liability claims across 25.3 million miles driven. The study found that Waymo vehicles had an 88% reduction in property damage claims and a 92% reduction in bodily injury claims compared to human-driver baselines. In real terms, Waymo vehicles were involved in only nine property damage claims and two bodily injury claims over that distance, whereas human drivers would be expected to incur 78 property damage claims and 26 bodily injury claims.
