# ai-reasoning-and-agents-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-2025-technical-performance|ai-index-2025-technical-performance]] — The AI Index Report 2025 analyzes technical performance across coding, math, and reasoning, highlighting records such as OpenAI's o3 achieving 97.9% on the MATH dataset and 87.7% on the GPQA diamond set, while noting the saturation of benchmarks like GSM8K.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, analyzes advancements in AI reasoning and agentic capabilities.

Regarding general, multisubject AI reasoning, researchers from NYU, Anthropic, and Meta introduced the GPQA benchmark to test AI systems. The GPQA dataset consists of 448 difficult multiple-choice questions crafted by subject-matter experts in fields such as biology, physics, and chemistry, designed to be resistant to simple web searches. In the 2023 AI Index, the best-performing model, GPT-4, achieved a score of 38.8% on the GPQA diamond test set (the most challenging subset and the one most frequently tested by AI developers). By December 2024, OpenAI's o3 model achieved a state-of-the-art score of 87.7% on the diamond set, a 48.9 percentage point improvement over the 2023 state-of-the-art. OpenAI's o3 was the first model to exceed the baseline accuracy of 81.3% set by expert human validators.

To evaluate the ability of AI systems to generalize beyond prior training, François Chollet (creator of the Keras deep learning library) introduced the ARC-AGI benchmark in 2019. ARC-AGI presents AI systems with independent tasks featuring demonstration or input pairs followed by test or output scenarios, requiring assumed prior knowledge (such as basic topology, objects, and elementary arithmetic) rather than specialized world knowledge. In 2020, the top-performing system on ARC-AGI scored 20%, and by 2024, the score rose to 33%. However, OpenAI's o3 model achieved a score of 75.7%. When o3 was allocated a high compute budget exceeding the benchmark's $10,000 limit, it reached a score of 87.5%. Researchers suggest that previous slow progress was due to an overemphasis on scaling model size and training data, whereas recent gains indicate a shift toward improving search capabilities and generalization.

Due to the saturation of benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval, the team behind MMLU introduced Humanity's Last Exam (HLE), a benchmark comprising 2,700 highly challenging multimodal questions across dozens of subject areas. These questions were contributed by leading professors and graduate-level reviewers and were specifically tested against state-of-the-art LLMs before inclusion to ensure no existing model could answer them. Initial testing shows HLE is extremely difficult; for example, OpenAI's o1 scores only 8.8%, while other models such as Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking (7.2%), Gemini 1.5 Pro (5.2%), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (4.8%), Grok-2 (3.9%), and GPT-4o (3.1%) score even lower. Researchers speculate performance could exceed 50% by the end of 2025.

In the domain of planning—reasoning about actions that alter the world—Arizona State University proposed PlanBench, a suite of 600 problems based on the International Planning Competition. PlanBench tests models on tasks such as constructing stacks of blocks (Blocksworld), where a hand can only move one block at a time. In the Blocksworld zero-shot evaluation, OpenAI's o1 scored 97.8%, significantly outperforming Llama 3.1 405B (62.6%) and GPT-4o (35.5%). In the more difficult Mystery Blocksworld domain, where answers are syntactically obfuscated, o1 scored 52.8%, while Llama 3.1 405B scored 0.8% and GPT-4 scored 0%. Despite these gains, o1 solves only 23.6% of planning instances that require at least 20 steps.

Additionally, the report discusses AI agents—autonomous or semiautonomous systems designed to accomplish goals in specific environments. Because agentic tasks are diverse and dynamic, they are harder to benchmark than image classification. In 2024, VisualAgentBench (VAB) was launched to test agents in three categories: embodied agents (household and gaming), GUI agents (mobile and web apps), and visual design agents (CSS debugging). On the VAB test set, GPT-4o achieved the highest overall success rate at 36.2%, while most proprietary models averaged around 20%.

To test the R&D capabilities of AI systems, researchers launched RE-Bench in 2024, featuring seven open-ended machine learning research environments, such as optimizing a kernel or fine-tuning GPT-2. In short time-horizon settings (two-hour budget), the best AI systems score four times higher than human experts. However, in a 32-hour budget setting, humans outperform AI by a factor of two. Some tasks, such as writing custom Triton kernels, can be completed by AI agents more quickly than by any human expert.
