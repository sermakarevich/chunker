# ai-index-report-2025-introduction

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis|ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis]] — The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025 by Stanford HAI details a record $252.3 billion in global private AI investment and a 280-fold decrease in GPT-3.5 level inference costs, while noting that the top two frontier AI models now differ by only 0.7% in performance.

The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published in April 2025 by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, is the eighth edition of the report. This 2025 Index is described as the most comprehensive to date, adding in-depth analyses of AI hardware, estimates of inference costs, and analyses of AI publication and patenting trends, as well as data on corporate adoption of responsible AI (RAI) practices and expanded coverage of AI's role in science and medicine. The AI Index was founded in 2017 as an offshoot of the One Hundred Year Study of Artificial Intelligence to provide policymakers, journalists, executives, researchers, and the public with rigorously validated, globally sourced data to inform decisions regarding the development and deployment of AI.

In the message from Co-directors Yolanda Gil and Raymond Perrault, they note that 2024 was a pivotal year where AI's role in advancing knowledge was recognized by Nobel prizes in physics and chemistry and the Turing award for reinforcement learning. They report that the Turing Test is no longer considered an ambitious goal because today's sophisticated systems have surpassed it. Corporate investment in AI rebounded in 2024, with the number of newly funded generative AI startups nearly tripling and business adoption accelerating significantly. Governments have also increased involvement, with several countries launching billion-dollar national AI infrastructure initiatives, including efforts to expand energy capacity.

Despite these advances, trust remains a challenge; fewer people believe AI companies will safeguard data, and concerns about fairness and bias persist. While skepticism remains, a 2024 global survey showed a rise in optimism about AI's potential for societal benefits. 

Key takeaways from the report include:
1. AI performance on demanding benchmarks improved sharply in 2024. Scores on MMMU, GPQA, and SWE-bench rose by 18.8, 48.9, and 67.3 percentage points, respectively, compared to 2023. AI also made strides in high-quality video generation and programming tasks.
2. AI is increasingly integrated into daily life. The FDA approved 223 AI-enabled medical devices in 2023, compared to six in 2015. In transportation, Waymo provides over 150,000 autonomous rides per week in the U.S., and Baidu's Apollo Go robotaxi fleet serves numerous Chinese cities.
3. Business investment and usage are rising. U.S. private AI investment grew to $109.1 billion in 2024, which is nearly 12 times China's $9.3 billion and 24 times the U.K.'s $4.5 billion. Generative AI attracted $33.9 billion in global private investment (an 18.7% increase from 2023), and 78% of organizations reported using AI in 2024, up from 55% the previous year.
4. The U.S. leads in producing top AI models (40 notable models in 2024), but China is closing the performance gap on benchmarks like MMLU and HumanEval, moving from double-digit differences in 2023 to near parity in 2024. China continues to lead in AI publications and patents.
5. The responsible AI (RAI) ecosystem is evolving unevenly. While AI-related incidents are rising and standardized RAI evaluations are rare, new benchmarks like HELM Safety, AIR-Bench, and FACTS assess factuality and safety. In 2024, the OECD, EU, U.N., and African Union released frameworks for transparency and trustworthiness.
6. Global optimism varies by region. Majorities in China (83%), Indonesia (80%), and Thailand (77%) view AI as beneficial. Optimism is lower in Canada (40%), the United States (39%), and the Netherlands (36%), though sentiment has improved since 2022 in Germany (+10%), France (+10%), Canada (+8%), Great Britain (+8%), and the United States (+4%).
7. AI is becoming more efficient and affordable. The inference cost for a system performing at the level of GPT-3.5 dropped over 280-fold between November 2022 and October 2024. Hardware costs declined by 30% annually, and energy efficiency improved by 40% annually. The performance gap between open-weight and closed models shrank from 8% to 1.7% on some benchmarks in one year.
8. Government regulation and investment are increasing. U.S. federal agencies introduced 59 AI-related regulations in 2024, more than double the 2023 count. Global legislative mentions of AI rose 21.3% across 75 countries since 2023. Major investments include Canada ($2.4 billion), China ($47.5 billion semiconductor fund), France (€109 billion), India ($1.25 billion), and Saudi Arabia's Project Transcendence ($100 billion).
9. AI and computer science (CS) education is expanding. Two-thirds of countries now offer or plan to offer K–12 CS education, and U.S. bachelor's degrees in computing increased 22% over 10 years. However, 81% of U.S. K–12 CS teachers believe AI should be part of the curriculum, but less than half feel equipped to teach it.
10. Industry dominates model production, with nearly 90% of notable AI models in 2024 coming from industry, compared to 60% in 2023. Training compute doubles every five months and datasets every eight months. The Elo skill score difference between the top and 10th-ranked models fell from 11.9% to 5.4% in a year, while the top two are separated by only 0.7%.
11. AI's impact on science is evidenced by two Nobel Prizes for work leading to deep learning and protein folding, and a Turing Award for reinforcement learning.
12. Complex reasoning remains a challenge, as AI models still struggle with benchmarks like PlanBench and frequently fail logic tasks where correct solutions exist.

The report was produced by the AI Index Steering Committee, including Chair Raymond Perrault and Chair-elect Jack Clark, and an editorial team led by Research Manager and Editor-in-Chief Nestor Maslej. Raw data and charts are available on Google Drive and the Global AI Vibrancy Tool allows for the comparison of AI ecosystems across over 30 countries.
