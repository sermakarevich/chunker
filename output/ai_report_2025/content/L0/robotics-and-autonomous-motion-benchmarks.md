# robotics-and-autonomous-motion-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-robotics-autonomous-motion-performance|ai-robotics-autonomous-motion-performance]] — The AI Index Report 2025 details advancements in AI assistants (GAIA benchmark scores rising to 65.1%), humanoid robotics (Figure 02 and GR00T), and autonomous vehicles, noting that Waymo vehicles show an 88% reduction in property damage claims compared to human drivers over 25.3 million miles.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, details several benchmarks and advancements in AI reasoning, robotics, and autonomous vehicles.

Regarding general AI assistants, Meta introduced the GAIA benchmark in May 2024. GAIA consists of 466 questions designed to assess the ability of AI systems to perform a broad range of tasks, including reasoning, multimodal processing, web browsing, and tool use. Unlike exam-style questions, GAIA challenges AI models with complex, multistep problems that may require searching the open web, interpreting multimodal inputs, and reasoning through intricate scenarios. When GAIA was launched, researchers found that existing Large Language Models (LLMs) lagged significantly behind human performance; for example, GPT-4 with plugins correctly answered only 15% of the questions, compared to 92% for human respondents. By 2024, the top system on GAIA achieved a score of 65.1%, which is a roughly 30 percentage point increase over the highest score recorded in 2023.

In the field of robotics, the Robot Learning Benchmark (RLBench), launched in 2019, is one of the most widely adopted benchmarks and features 100 unique tasks ranging from simple target reaching to opening an oven and placing a tray inside. Researchers typically evaluate systems on a standardized subset of 18 tasks. As of January 2025, the top-performing model on this subset is SAM2Act—a collaboration between the University of Washington, Universidad Católica San Pablo, Nvidia, and the Allen Institute for AI—which achieved an 86.8% success rate. This represents a 2.8 percentage point improvement over the 2024 state-of-the-art and a 66.7 percentage point increase from the leading score in 2021.

Humanoid robotics saw significant growth in 2024. Figure AI launched Figure 02, a general-purpose humanoid robot standing 5 feet 6 inches tall, weighing 154 pounds, and capable of handling a 44-pound payload. Figure 02 can operate for up to five hours on a single charge and can perform tasks such as making coffee and assisting in automotive assembly by placing sheet metal into a car fixture. Integrated with OpenAI, Figure 02 can engage in speech-to-speech reasoning to explain its actions and respond to queries. Other notable humanoid robots include Tesla’s Optimus (first launched in 2002 and redesigned in 2023) and Boston Dynamics’ Atlas.

Google DeepMind also made several robotic advancements. In 2023, DeepMind launched PaLM-E and RT-2, which used transformer-based architectures and training on both manipulation and language data to excel at both robotic manipulation and text generation. In 2024, DeepMind introduced AutoRT, an AI system that uses foundation models to autonomously generate training data by coordinating multiple video-equipped robots; AutoRT has generated a dataset of 77,000 robotic trials across 6,650 unique tasks. Additionally, DeepMind developed SARA-RT, which uses a technique called "up-training" to convert the quadratic complexity of standard transformers into a linear model, reducing computational demands while maintaining performance. DeepMind also developed the ALOHA (Autonomous and DemoStart Learning of High-level Activities) approach, enabling robots to perform intricate tasks such as tying shoelaces, hanging a shirt, and stacking kitchen items. DemoStart, another DeepMind method, is an auto-curriculum reinforcement learning method that allows robotic arms to master complex behaviors using sparse rewards and limited demonstrations. Furthermore, DeepMind introduced a robotic model in 2024 capable of reaching amateur human-level performance in competitive table tennis.

In 2024, there was a significant push toward robotic foundation models. Nvidia introduced GR00T (Generalist Robot 00 Technology), a general-purpose foundation model for humanoid robots designed to mimic human movements and understand natural language, along with data pipelines, simulation frameworks, and the Thor robotics computer. Other robotic foundation models include Covariant's RFM-1, which features language reasoning and real-world maneuverability, and LLaRA, developed by researchers at Stony Brook University and the University of Wisconsin-Madison, which integrates perception, communication, and action into a single deep learning model. These follow the 2023 trend of models like RT-2, PaLM-E, and Open-X Embodiment.

Regarding self-driving cars, Waymo (a subsidiary of Alphabet) operates robotaxis in Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Austin. As of January 2025, Waymo provides 150,000 paid rides per week, covering over a million miles. Waymo has driven the following rider-only miles through September 2024: 20.823 million in Phoenix, 10.209 million in San Francisco, 1.947 million in Los Angeles, and 124,000 in Austin. Waymo also plans to test vehicles in 10 additional cities, including Las Vegas, San Diego, and Miami, and has tested in snowy conditions in upstate New York and Truckee, California. In China, Baidu’s Apollo Go reported 988,000 rides in Q3 2024, a 20% year-over-year increase; as of October 2024, Apollo Go operated 400 robotaxis with plans to expand to 1,000 by the end of 2025. Pony.AI also plans to scale its fleet from 200 to between 2,000 and 3,000 vehicles by the end of 2026. China is currently testing more driverless cars than any other country and is rolling them out across 16 cities. In Europe, startups like Wayve are also gaining traction.
