# ai-index-2025-report-highlights

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis|ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis]] — The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025 by Stanford HAI details a record $252.3 billion in global private AI investment and a 280-fold decrease in GPT-3.5 level inference costs, while noting that the top two frontier AI models now differ by only 0.7% in performance.

The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides the following highlights across research, technical performance, economy, and responsible AI.

### Chapter 1: Research and Development
1. Industry continues to make significant investments in AI and leads in notable AI model development, while academia leads in highly cited research. Industry’s lead in notable model development has grown more pronounced, with nearly 90% of notable models in 2024 originating from industry, compared to 60% in 2023. Academia has remained the single leading institutional producer of highly cited (top 100) publications over the past three years.
2. China leads in AI research publication totals, while the United States leads in highly influential research. In 2023, China produced more AI publications (23.2%) and citations (22.6%) than any other country. Over the past three years, U.S. institutions have contributed the most top-100-cited AI publications.
3. AI publication totals continue to grow and increasingly dominate computer science. Between 2013 and 2023, the total number of AI publications in venues related to computer science and other scientific disciplines nearly tripled, increasing from approximately 102,000 to over 242,000. Proportionally, AI’s share of computer science publications rose from 21.6% in 2013 to 41.8% in 2023.
4. The United States continues to be the leading source of notable AI models. In 2024, U.S.-based institutions produced 40 notable AI models, significantly surpassing China’s 15 and Europe’s combined total of three. In the past decade, more notable machine learning models have originated from the United States than any other country.
5. AI models are becoming larger, more computationally demanding, and more energy intensive. Training compute for notable AI models doubles approximately every five months, dataset sizes for training Large Language Models (LLMs) double every eight months, and the power required for training doubles annually.
6. AI models are becoming increasingly cheaper to use. The cost of querying an AI model that scores the equivalent of GPT-3.5 (64.8) on MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) dropped from $20.00 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 per million tokens by October 2024 (Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B), representing a more than 280-fold reduction in approximately 18 months. Depending on the task, LLM inference prices have fallen between 9 and 900 times per year.
7. AI patenting is on the rise. Between 2010 and 2023, the number of AI patents grew from 3,833 to 122,511, with a 29.6% increase in the last year. As of 2023, China leads in total AI patents, accounting for 69.7% of all grants, while South Korea and Luxembourg are top AI patent producers on a per capita basis.
8. AI hardware is getting faster, cheaper, and more energy efficient. Machine learning hardware performance (measured in 16-bit floating-point operations) has grown 43% annually, doubling every 1.9 years. Costs have dropped 30% per year, while energy efficiency has increased by 40% annually.
9. Carbon emissions from AI training are increasing. Training AlexNet (2012) emitted 0.01 tons of carbon, whereas GPT-3 (2020) emitted 588 tons, GPT-4 (2023) emitted 5,184 tons, and Llama 3.1 405B (2024) emitted 8,930 tons. For comparison, the average American emits 18 tons of carbon per year.

### Chapter 2: Technical Performance
1. AI is mastering new benchmarks faster than ever. Performance on benchmarks introduced in 2023—MMMU, GPQA, and SWE-bench—saw remarkable improvements by 2024. Gains were 18.8 and 48.9 percentage points on MMMU and GPQA, respectively. On SWE-bench, AI systems improved from solving 4.4% of coding problems in 2023 to 71.7% in 2024.
2. Open-weight models are catching up to closed-weight models. In early January 2024, the leading closed-weight model outperformed the top open-weight model by 8.0% on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard; by February 2025, this gap narrowed to 1.7%.
3. The gap is closing between Chinese and U.S. models. At the end of 2023, performance gaps on benchmarks such as MMLU, MMMU, MATH, and HumanEval were 17.5, 13.5, 24.3, and 31.6 percentage points, respectively. By the end of 2024, these margins narrowed to 0.3, 8.1, 1.6, and 3.7 percentage points.
4. AI model performance is converging at the frontier. The Elo score difference between the top and 10th-ranked model on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard narrowed from 11.9% (per the 2024 AI Index) to 5.4% by early 2025. The difference between the top two models shrank from 4.9% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024.
5. New reasoning paradigms, such as test-time compute, improve performance. OpenAI's o1 and o3 models (introduced in 2024) use iterative reasoning to dramatically improve results. The o1 model scored 74.4% on an International Mathematical Olympiad qualifying exam, compared to GPT-4o’s 9.3%. However, o1 is nearly six times more expensive and 30 times slower than GPT-4o.
6. More challenging benchmarks are being proposed due to the saturation of traditional ones like MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval. New evaluations include Humanity’s Last Exam (top system scores 8.80%), FrontierMath (AI solves only 2%), and BigCodeBench (AI systems achieve 35.5% success, compared to the human standard of 97%).
7. High-quality AI video generators showed significant improvement in 2024 with the launch of OpenAI’s SORA, Stable Video Diffusion 3D and 4D, Meta’s Movie Gen, and Google DeepMind’s Veo 2.
8. Smaller models are driving stronger performance. In 2022, the smallest model scoring over 60% on MMLU was PaLM (540 billion parameters). By 2024, Microsoft’s Phi-3-mini (3.8 billion parameters) achieved the same threshold, a 142-fold reduction in size.
9. Complex reasoning remains a problem. Despite mechanisms like chain-of-thought reasoning, LLMs cannot reliably solve problems requiring logical reasoning (e.g., arithmetic and planning) on instances larger than those they were trained on.
10. AI agents show early promise. According to the RE-Bench benchmark introduced in 2024, top AI systems score four times higher than human experts in short time-horizon settings (two-hour budget). However, in a 32-hour budget setting, human performance surpasses AI two to one. AI agents match human expertise in select tasks, such as writing Triton kernels.

### Chapter 3: Responsible AI
1. Evaluating AI systems with Responsible AI (RAI) criteria is still uncommon, but new benchmarks like HELM Safety and AIR-Bench are emerging to fill the gap in standardized RAI benchmarks for LLMs.
2. AI incident reports are increasing. The AI Incidents Database reports that AI-related incidents rose to 233 in 2024, a 56.4% increase over 2023.
3. Organizations acknowledge RAI risks, but mitigation efforts lag. A McKinsey survey shows that inaccuracy (64%), regulatory compliance (63%), and cybersecurity (60%) were top concerns for leaders, but not all are taking active steps to address them.
4. Policymakers are showing significant interest in RAI. In 2024, the OECD, European Union, United Nations, and African Union published frameworks for transparency, explainability, and trustworthiness.
5. The data commons is shrinking. In actively maintained domains in the C4 common crawl dataset, the proportion of restricted tokens jumped from 5–7% to 20–33% between 2023 and 2024 as websites implement protocols to curb data scraping.
6. Foundation model research transparency is improving. The Foundation Model Transparency Index showed the average transparency score among major model developers increased from 37% in October 2023 to 58% in May 2024.
7. New benchmarks for factuality and truthfulness have emerged, such as the updated Hughes Hallucination Evaluation Model leaderboard, FACTS, and SimpleQA, because earlier benchmarks like HaluEval and TruthfulQA failed to gain widespread adoption.
8. AI-related election misinformation spread globally in 2024 across more than a dozen countries and over 10 social media platforms, including during the U.S. presidential election, though its measurable impact remains unclear.
9. LLMs trained to be unbiased continue to exhibit implicit bias. Models such as GPT-4 and Claude 3 Sonnet disproportionately associate negative terms with Black individuals, associate women with humanities rather than STEM, and favor men for leadership roles.
10. Academic research into RAI is growing. The number of RAI papers accepted at leading AI conferences increased by 28.8%, from 992 in 2023 to 1,278 in 2024.

### Chapter 4: Economy
1. Global private AI investment reached a record high of $252.3 billion in 2024, representing a 26% growth. Private investment climbed 44.5% and mergers and acquisitions rose 12.1% from the previous year. Total investment has grown more than thirteenfold since 2014.
2. Private investment in generative AI specifically reached $33.9 billion in 2024, an 18.7% increase from 2023 and more than 8.5 times the 2022 levels.
