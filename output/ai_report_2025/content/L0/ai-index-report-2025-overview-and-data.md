# ai-index-report-2025-overview-and-data

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-2025-report-synthesis-and-technical-timeline|ai-index-2025-report-synthesis-and-technical-timeline]] — The AI Index Report 2025 documents 2024's trends including U.S. AI investment of $109.1 billion, SWE-bench scores rising 67.3 percentage points, inference costs dropping 280-fold, and a chronological timeline of 32 model releases featuring GPT-4o, Llama 3.1 405B, and o3, with ARC-AGI records reaching 87.5% and open-weight models closing the gap to closed models from 8% to 1.7%.

The AI Index Report 2025 is the eighth edition of the annual AI Index report. It is the most comprehensive edition to date and examines AI’s intensifying influence on society, the economy, and global governance. This year’s report includes in-depth analyses of the evolving AI hardware landscape, novel estimates of inference costs, updated AI publication and patenting trends, corporate adoption of responsible AI practices, and expanded coverage of AI in science and medicine. The AI Index was founded in 2017 as an offshoot of the One Hundred Year Study of Artificial Intelligence. Its mission is to equip policymakers, journalists, executives, researchers, and the public with accurate, rigorously validated, and globally sourced data to inform AI development and deployment decisions. The report tracks geopolitical shifts, technological evolution, and AI’s expanding role in business and public life. Recognized globally, the AI Index has been cited in The New York Times, Bloomberg, and The Guardian; referenced in hundreds of academic papers; utilized by worldwide policymakers and government agencies; and used to brief companies including Accenture, IBM, Wells Fargo, and Fidelity.

In a message from Co-directors Yolanda Gil and Raymond Perrault, the report notes that 2024 highlighted AI’s accelerating adoption and integration into daily professional and leisure activities. Nobel prizes in physics and chemistry, alongside the Turing Award for reinforcement learning, recognized AI’s role in advancing human knowledge. The Turing Test is no longer considered an ambitious goal for current systems. Corporate investment rebounded after a slowdown, with newly funded generative AI startups nearly tripling and business adoption accelerating significantly. Governments launched billion-dollar national AI infrastructure initiatives, including energy capacity expansions, while global coordination increased. Despite rising trust challenges—fewer people believe AI companies protect their data, and concerns over fairness, bias, and election deepfakes persist—public optimism rose in a 2024 global survey.

The report’s Top Takeaways detail specific metrics and trends:
1. AI performance on demanding benchmarks improved sharply: scores on the 2023-introduced benchmarks MMMU, GPQA, and SWE-bench rose by 18.8, 48.9, and 67.3 percentage points, respectively, within a year. AI systems advanced video generation, and language model agents outperformed humans in time-constrained programming tasks.
2. AI integration into daily life accelerated: the FDA approved 223 AI-enabled medical devices in 2023 (up from six in 2015). Waymo provided over 150,000 autonomous rides weekly, and Baidu’s Apollo Go robotaxi fleet expanded across Chinese cities.
3. Business investment and usage surged: U.S. private AI investment reached $109.1 billion in 2024 (compared to China’s $9.3 billion and the U.K.’s $4.5 billion). Generative AI attracted $33.9 billion globally in private investment, an 18.7% increase from 2023. AI business usage reached 78% of organizations in 2024 (up from 55% in 2023), with research confirming productivity gains and skill gap narrowing.
4. The U.S. leads in top AI model production: U.S. institutions produced 40 notable models in 2024 versus China’s 15 and Europe’s three. Chinese models closed the quality gap on MMLU and HumanEval from double-digit deficits in 2023 to near parity in 2024. China leads in AI publications and patents. Global development expanded to the Middle East, Latin America, and Southeast Asia.
5. Responsible AI governance evolved unevenly: AI incidents rose, and standardized responsible AI evaluations remain rare among developers. New benchmarks (HELM Safety, AIR-Bench, FACTS) address factuality and safety. Governments intensified 2024 regulatory cooperation via the OECD, EU, U.N., and African Union.
6. Global AI optimism varies by region: China (83%), Indonesia (80%), and Thailand (77%) show high optimism, while Canada (40%), the U.S. (39%), and the Netherlands (36%) remain lower. Since 2022, optimism grew in Germany (+10%), France (+10%), Canada (+8%), Great Britain (+8%), and the U.S. (+4%).
7. AI efficiency and accessibility improved dramatically: Inference costs for a system matching GPT-3.5 performance dropped over 280-fold between November 2022 and October 2024. Hardware costs declined 30% annually, energy efficiency improved 40% annually, and open-weight models closed the performance gap with closed models from 8% to 1.7% on some benchmarks in one year.
8. Government regulation and investment expanded: U.S. federal agencies introduced 59 AI-related regulations in 2024 (double 2023’s count and issued by twice as many agencies). Global legislative AI mentions rose 21.3% across 75 countries since 2023 (a ninefold increase since 2016). Major investments include Canada’s $2.4 billion, China’s $47.5 billion semiconductor fund, France’s €109 billion, India’s $1.25 billion, and Saudi Arabia’s $100 billion Project Transcendence.
9. AI and computer science education expanded but faces access gaps: Two-thirds of countries now offer or plan K–12 computer science education (double the 2019 count), with Africa and Latin America leading progress. U.S. computing bachelor’s graduates increased 22% over 10 years. African access remains limited by infrastructure gaps like electricity. In the U.S., 81% of K–12 CS teachers advocate for AI in foundational education, but fewer than half feel equipped to teach it.
10. Industry dominates model development while the frontier tightens: Nearly 90% of notable 2024 AI models came from industry (up from 60% in 2023), though academia leads in highly cited research. Model scale grew rapidly with training compute doubling every five months and datasets every eight months. Performance gaps narrowed: the Elo skill score difference between the top and 10th-ranked models fell from 11.9% to 5.4% in a year, and the top two models separated by just 0.7%.
11. AI earned major scientific honors: Two Nobel Prizes recognized deep learning (physics) and protein folding applications (chemistry), while the Turing Award honored reinforcement learning contributions.
12. Complex reasoning remains difficult for AI: While models excel at International Mathematical Olympiad problems, they struggle with PlanBench and often fail logic tasks despite the existence of provably correct solutions, limiting high-stakes utility.
