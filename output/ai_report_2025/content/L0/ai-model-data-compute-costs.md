# ai-model-data-compute-costs

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-research-patents-models-trends|ai-research-patents-models-trends]] — The AI Index Report 2025 reveals a China-led surge in AI research publications (23.2% share) and patents (69.7% share) by 2023, while the U.S. leads in notable AI models (40 in 2024) and top-cited research. The report also highlights a training compute doubling every five months and a massive reduction in inference costs, such as GPT-3.5 level performance costs falling from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens.

The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides detailed analysis on the development and costs of notable AI models. 

Regarding training compute, the launch of DeepSeek’s V3 model in December 2024 gained significant attention because it achieved high performance while requiring fewer computational resources than many leading Large Language Models (LLMs). According to Epoch AI, top 10 Chinese language models have scaled their training compute at a rate of approximately three times per year since late 2021, which is considerably slower than the five times per year trend observed in the rest of the world since 2018. Top-tier AI models from the United States have generally been more computationally intensive than those from China.

There is an ongoing debate regarding whether AI models will run out of training data. One key driver of algorithmic improvements has been the scaling of models on ever-larger datasets, but concerns have grown about a potential data bottleneck as internet training data becomes depleted. New research suggests that current data stocks may last longer than previously expected. Epoch AI estimates the total effective stock of data available for training models by token count as follows: Common Crawl contains a median of 130 trillion tokens, the indexed web holds approximately 510 trillion tokens, and the entire web (including private data) contains around 3,100 trillion tokens. Additionally, the stock of images is estimated at 300 trillion and video at 1,350 trillion. Epoch AI projects, with an 80% confidence interval, that the current stock of training data will be fully utilized between 2026 and 2032. This timeline is influenced by the historical growth of dataset sizes and computer usage. If models are trained in a compute-optimal manner, the data stock lasts longer; however, if models are overtrained—trained beyond the point of diminishing returns to achieve more compute-efficient inference—the stock may be depleted more quickly.

These projections revise earlier estimates from Epoch AI, which predicted high-quality text data would be depleted by 2024. The update reflects research showing that web data performs better than curated corpora and that models can be trained on the same datasets multiple times. While using synthetic data (data generated by AI models) as a solution to data shortages has been suggested, the 2024 AI Index suggests that models trained on repeated cycles of synthetic data likely lose representation of the tails of distributions, leading to degraded output quality (model collapse). However, when synthetic data is layered on top of real data rather than replacing it, the collapse phenomenon does not occur, although it does not necessarily improve performance or reduce test loss. 

Advances have been made in generating high-fidelity synthetic data, though it remains generally distinguishable from real data. A team of Slovenian researchers found that most synthetic relational data methods are detectable and typically deteriorate in performance compared to real data, though some methods like using Synthetic Data Vault (SDV) vs. Walmart data to train an XGBoost classifier showed synthetic data could outperform real data in some cases. In the healthcare domain, some model architectures using synthetic data have shown enhanced performance, increasing F1 scores or AUROC by 5%–10% on minority classes. To improve factuality, researchers from Stanford and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill introduced FactTune-FS methods, which have tended to outperform other RLHF and decoding-based methods. 

As web content becomes increasingly AI-generated, future models will inevitably be trained on non-human material. While synthetic data and data augmentation (modifying real data through tilting or image mixing) offer a near-infinite supply, their effective use requires further research into learning dynamics.

In terms of inference costs—the expense of querying a trained model, typically measured in USD per million tokens—the AI Index, working with Epoch AI, found that costs for a fixed AI performance threshold have decreased substantially. For an AI model scoring the equivalent of GPT-3.5 (64.8 on MMLU), the cost dropped from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 per million tokens by October 2024 (Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B), a more than 280-fold reduction. For models scoring above 50% on GPQA, costs declined from $15 per million tokens in May 2024 to $0.12 per million tokens by December 2024 (Phi 4). Epoch AI estimates that LLM inference costs have fallen between nine and 900 times per year depending on the task. While top-tier models from developers such as OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic remain more expensive than smaller models, reflecting a premium for cutting-edge performance, the general trend shows AI performance per dollar has improved substantially.
