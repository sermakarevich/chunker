# ai-index-2025-public-opinion-research-trends

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis|ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis]] — The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025 by Stanford HAI details a record $252.3 billion in global private AI investment and a 280-fold decrease in GPT-3.5 level inference costs, while noting that the top two frontier AI models now differ by only 0.7% in performance.

According to the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, global perspectives on AI's impact are varied. Globally, 60% of respondents agree that AI will change how individuals do their jobs in the next five years, while 36% believe that AI will replace their jobs in that same timeframe. Regarding daily life, 55% of respondents believe AI will save time and 51% expect it will offer better entertainment options. However, confidence in other benefits is lower: only 38% believe AI will improve health, 36% think AI will improve the national economy, 31% see a positive impact on the job market, and 37% believe AI will enhance their own jobs.

In the United States, local policymakers show sharp divides regarding AI policy priorities. While these local U.S. policymakers broadly support AI regulation, their specific priorities vary. There is strong backing for stricter data privacy rules (80.4%), retraining for the unemployed (76.2%), and AI deployment regulations (72.5%). In contrast, support drops significantly for a law enforcement facial recognition ban (34.2%), wage subsidies for wage declines (32.9%), and universal basic income (24.6%).

Additionally, the report's first chapter, "Research and Development," explores trends in AI research and development through the analysis of AI publications, patents, and notable AI systems across different countries, organizations, and sectors. This chapter also covers AI model training costs, AI conference attendance, and open-source AI software. New additions to the 2025 report include profiles of the evolving AI hardware ecosystem, an assessment of AI training's energy requirements and environmental impact, and a temporal analysis of model inference costs.

Within the "Research and Development" chapter, the highlights include that industry leads in notable AI model development—with nearly 90% of notable models in 2024 originating from industry compared to 60% in 2023—while academia leads in highly cited research. China leads in AI research publication totals, with 23.2% of publications and 22.6% of citations in 2023, whereas the United States leads in highly influential research. Between 2013 and 2023, the total number of AI publications in computer science and other scientific disciplines rose from approximately 102,000 to over 242,000, with AI's share of computer science publications increasing from 21.6% in 2013 to 41.8% in 2023. The United States remains the leading source of notable AI models, producing 40 in 2024, compared to China's 15 and Europe's combined total of three. 

Technical trends in model development show that training compute for notable AI models doubles every five months, dataset sizes for training Large Language Models (LLMs) double every eight months, and power requirements double annually. Regarding costs, the price of querying an AI model equivalent to GPT-3.5 (64.8 on MMLU) dropped from $20.00 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 per million tokens by October 2024 (Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B). AI patenting is also increasing; between 2010 and 2023, the number of AI patents grew from 3,833 to 122,511, with a 29.6% increase in the last year. As of 2023, China leads in total AI patents, accounting for 69.7% of all grants. Hardware performance, measured in 16-bit floating-point operations, grows 43% annually, while costs drop 30% per year and energy efficiency increases by 40% annually. Finally, carbon emissions from training have risen significantly, from 0.01 tons for AlexNet (2012) to 588 tons for GPT-3 (2020), 5,184 tons for GPT-4 (2023), and 8,930 tons for Llama 3.1 405B (2024).
