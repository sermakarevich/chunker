# rai-organizational-trends-and-academic-research-2025

**Parent:** [[content/L1/responsible-ai-benchmarks-incidents-and-governance|responsible-ai-benchmarks-incidents-and-governance]] — The AI Index Report 2025 documents a record 233 responsible AI incidents in 2024, up 56.4%, including high-profile cases like the Elliston Berry deepfake harassment and Sewell Setzer III chatbot tragedy, while revealing significant disparities in RAI adoption where McKinsey data shows 66% of firms recognize cybersecurity risks but only 60% mitigate them, and benchmark standardization remains fragmented with safety tests like BBQ and HarmBench covering distinct model subsets.

Data from the Accenture/Stanford Joint Survey, conducted in January–February 2025, collected responses from 1,500 organizations with at least $500 million in annual revenues across 20 countries and 19 industries. The survey aimed to assess challenges in adopting Responsible AI (RAI) principles, highlight the urgent need to prioritize AI system security and robust data governance, and compare RAI activities across 10 dimensions over time. Because the survey was conducted in both 2024 and 2025, it enables a longitudinal comparison of organizational perspectives on RAI adoption. In the past two years, organizations reported the following AI-related incident types: adversarial attacks (56%), privacy violations (55%), unintended decision making (51%), model bias (47%), and performance failures (46%). With 51% of respondents reporting unintended decision making and 47% citing model bias, many organizations struggle to anticipate and control AI behavior, a critical challenge in high-stakes environments.

Regarding the relevance of specific RAI risks, reported percentages for 2025 versus 2024 were: privacy and data-related risks (65% vs. 51%), reliability risks (45% vs. 56%), compliance and lawfulness risks (29% vs. 52%), security risks (47% vs. 50%), financial risks (12% vs. 42%), brand and reputational risks (26% vs. 35%), human interaction risks (40% vs. 33%), diversity and nondiscrimination risks (29% vs. 32%), client and customer risks (30% vs. 34%), societal risks (22% vs. 33%), and socio-environmental risks (20% vs. 30%). The analysis notes that companies have grown significantly more concerned about financial risks (+38 percentage points), brand and reputational risks (+16), privacy and data-related risks (+15), and reliability risks (+14) in recent years, while societal risks (-7) and socio-environmental risks (-8) are now considered less pressing.

Between 2024 and 2025, organizational RAI maturity advanced notably, with more companies securing CEO support and improving AI risk identification, monitoring, and control. Conversely, operational RAI maturity—focused on practical system-level safeguards like bias reduction, adversarial testing, and environmental impact measurement—lagged behind, highlighting a persistent disconnect between high-level strategic commitments and technical implementation. Organizational attitudes toward RAI remained evenly split across several domains. On the spectrum between 'innovate first, regulate later' and 'safety first, prevent future potential risks,' 64% of respondents lean toward safety. Views on whether RAI is a compliance issue or a value driver were divided, with 17% aligning with compliance and 37% with value creation. Regarding risk ownership, 20% assign GenAI risk responsibility to foundation model providers and 33% to users. Opinions on model safety show 18% preferring closed-source models and 29% preferring open-source models. Finally, 21% of organizations are actively exploring minimally supervised AI agents, while 37% believe agents are currently too risky for large-scale adoption, illustrating the tension between safety prioritization (64%) and innovation exploration (58%).

In academic research, the AI Index analyzed RAI-related papers accepted at six leading conferences: AAAI, AIES, FAccT, ICML, ICLR, and NeurIPS. The total number of accepted RAI papers increased by 28.8%, rising from 992 in 2023 to 1,278 in 2024. The proportion of accepted papers dedicated to RAI varied by conference: FAccT (69.14%), AIES (63.33%), AAAI (13.36%), NeurIPS (9.02% in 2024, down from 13.8% in 2023), ICML (8.24% in 2024, up from 3.4% in 2023), and ICLR (7.56%). Geographically, the United States leads total RAI publications (2019–2024) with 3,158 papers, followed by China (1,100) and the United Kingdom (485). In 2024 specifically, the United States accounted for 669 papers, China 268, Germany 80, the United Kingdom 67, Canada 55, Hong Kong 46, India 42, Singapore 39, Japan 36, and the Netherlands 31. Topic-specific trends show that privacy and data governance papers decreased by 14.5% in 2024 but have risen nearly fivefold since 2019. Conversely, fairness and bias papers surged to 408 in 2024, approximately double the 2023 count, reflecting growing academic focus on equity and bias mitigation.
