# language-benchmarks-reasoning-models

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025|ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025]] — The AI Index Report 2025 highlights significant leaps in AI reasoning (OpenAI o1/o3), long-context retrieval (Gemini 1.5 Pro), and coding (o3 solving 71.7% of SWE-bench), while noting the saturation of early benchmarks like HumanEval.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, discusses several new benchmarks and model capabilities in the domain of language.

Regarding the Arena-Hard-Auto benchmark, more than 50% of its questions focus specifically on coding and debugging. The benchmark also features a style control leaderboard to assess how response style influences preference. According to data from LMSYS (2025), models such as o1-mini (92.0), o1-preview (90.4), and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (85.2) achieved top scores on Arena-Hard-Auto. The November variant of Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 3.5 is the top model on the style leaderboard.

WildBench, developed in 2024 by researchers from the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington, evaluates Large Language Models (LLMs) on challenging, real-world queries. The creators of WildBench note that existing evaluations have limitations: MMLU focuses on academic questions and does not assess open-ended, real-world problems, while benchmarks like LMSYS rely heavily on human oversight and lack consistency in evaluating all models with the same dataset. WildBench addresses these issues by providing an automated evaluation framework using diverse "in the wild" questions selected from over 1 million human-chatbot interactions. Currently, GPT-4o is the top-performing model on WildBench with an Elo score of 1227.1, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet with a score of 1215.4.

OpenAI's latest models, o1 and o3, represent a paradigm shift in advanced reasoning through their use of a chain-of-thought process and the ability to iteratively check answers, a process known as inference or test-time computation. This allows the models to break complex problems into smaller steps before executing them. For instance, when deciphering scrambled text, o1 provides a more thorough reasoning process than GPT-4. 

Comparing benchmarks, o1 outperforms GPT-4o with a 2.8-point gain on MMLU, 34.5 points on MATH, 26.7 points on GPQA Diamond, and 65.1 points on AIME 2024. Specifically, o1 scored 74.4% on AIME 2024, compared to 9.3% for GPT-4o. In terms of machine intelligence, o3 achieved an 87.5% accuracy rate on the ARC-AGI benchmark, surpassing the previous record of 55.5%. However, these capabilities increase costs and latency. GPT-4o costs $2.50 per 1 million input tokens and $10 per 1 million output tokens, whereas o1 costs $15 per 1 million input tokens and $60 per 1 million output tokens. Furthermore, o1 is approximately 40 times slower than GPT-4o, with a time to first token of 29.7 seconds compared to 0.72 seconds for GPT-4o. OpenAI released o1-preview to ChatGPT Plus and Teams users on September 12, 2024, and released the full version of o1 and the $200 monthly ChatGPT Pro subscription on December 5, 2024. The o3 model is currently only available to select researchers and developers via OpenAI’s safety testing program.

MixEval, launched by researchers at the National University of Singapore, Carnegie Mellon University, and the Allen Institute for AI, is a benchmark that combines real-world user queries (similar to Chatbot Arena) with ground-truth-based questions (similar to MMLU). The MixEval-Hard suite focuses on substantially harder queries. On the MixEval-Hard benchmark, OpenAI’s o1-preview achieved the highest score of 72.0, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet-0620 (68.1) and Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct (66.2), all of which were released in 2024.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a capability where LLMs integrate with retrieval mechanisms to retrieve relevant information from documents before generating a response. In September 2024, Anthropic introduced Contextual Retrieval to enhance RAG capabilities. New benchmarks for RAG include Ragnarok and CRAG, as well as FinanceBench for financial question answering.

The Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard evaluates the ability of LLMs to call functions or tools using over 2,000 question-function-answer pairs across languages like Python, Java, JavaScript, and REST API. The top model is watt-tool-70b (a fine-tuned variant of Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct), which achieved an overall accuracy of 74.31, followed by a November variant of GPT-4o with a score of 72.08. Top model accuracy on this benchmark improved by up to 50 points over the course of 2024.

Finally, the Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB), created by Hugging Face and Cohere in late 2022, evaluates how models convert data into numerical embedding vectors to capture semantic meanings. MTEB includes 58 datasets spanning 112 languages and eight embedding tasks, such as bitext mining.
