# ai-technical-performance-2024-highlights

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-technical-performance-2024-2025|ai-technical-performance-2024-2025]] — The Technical Performance chapter of the AI Index Report 2025 details how AI models in 2024 achieved unprecedented benchmark gains (e.g., SWE-bench rising from 4.4% to 71.7%) and closed the gap between open-weight and closed-weight models, as well as US and Chinese models (e.g., MMLU gap narrowing from 17.5 to 0.3 percentage points). It also highlights the lauch of advanced models like OpenAI's o1 and o3, and the lauch of Meta's Llama 3.1 405B.

The Technical Performance section of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides a comprehensive overview of AI advancements in 2024. This section begins with a high-level summary of AI technical progress, covering major AI-related launches, the state of AI capabilities, and key trends, including the rising performance of open-weight models, the convergence of frontier model performance, and the improving quality of Chinese Large Language Models (LLMs). The chapter further examines various AI capabilities, such as language understanding and generation, retrieval-augmented generation, coding, mathematics, reasoning, computer vision, speech, and agentic AI, while adding significantly expanded analyses of performance trends in robotics and self-driving cars.

Key highlights from the Technical Performance chapter include:

1. AI systems are mastering new benchmarks faster than ever. For benchmarks introduced in 2023 to test the limits of capable AI—namely MMMU, GPQA, and SWE-bench—performance saw remarkable improvements by 2024. Specifically, gains were 18.8 percentage points on MMMU and 48.9 percentage points on GPQA. On SWE-bench, AI systems' ability to solve coding problems jumped from 4.4% in 2023 to 71.7% in 2024.

2. Open-weight models are catching up to closed-weight models. While leading open-weight models lagged significantly in previous years, by 2024 the gap had nearly disappeared. In early January 2024, the leading closed-weight model outperformed the top open-weight model by 8.04% on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard, but by February 2025, this gap narrowed to 1.70%.

3. The performance gap between Chinese and US models is closing. In 2023, leading American models significantly outperformed Chinese models, but this trend shifted by the end of 2024. Performance gaps on benchmarks such as MMLU, MMMU, MATH, and HumanEval were 17.5, 13.5, 24.3, and 31.6 percentage points, respectively, at the end of 2023; by the end of 2024, these differences narrowed to 0.3, 8.1, 1.6, and 3.7 percentage points.

4. AI model performance is converging at the frontier. The Elo score difference between the top and 10th-ranked model on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard narrowed from 11.9% (as reported in the previous AI Index) to 5.4% by early 2025. Additionally, the difference between the top two models shrank from 4.9% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024, indicating an increasingly competitive landscape with high-quality models available from more developers.

5. New reasoning paradigms, such as test-time compute, improve model performance. In 2024, OpenAI introduced models o1 and o3 that use iterative reasoning to improve outputs. The o1 model scored 74.4% on an International Mathematical Olympiad qualifying exam, compared to 9.3% for GPT-4o. However, o1 is nearly six times more expensive and 30 times slower than GPT-4o.

6. Researchers are continually proposing more challenging benchmarks due to the saturation of traditional benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval. New evaluations include Humanity’s Last Exam, where the top system scores 8.80%; FrontierMath, where AI systems solve only 2% of problems; and BigCodeBench, where AI systems achieve a 35.5% success rate, compared to a human standard of 97%.

7. High-quality AI video generators demonstrated significant improvement in 2024. Notable releases included OpenAI’s SORA, Stable Video 3D and 4D, Meta’s Movie Gen, and Google DeepMind’s Veo 2, producing videos of significantly higher quality than those from 2023.

8. Smaller models are achieving stronger performance. In 2022, the smallest model to score over 60% on MMLU was PaLM with 540 billion parameters; by 2024, Microsoft’s Phi-3-mini achieved the same threshold with only 3.8 billion parameters, a 142-fold reduction in size.

9. Complex reasoning remains a persistent problem. Despite mechanisms like chain-of-thought reasoning, LLMs cannot reliably solve problems requiring logical reasoning, such as arithmetic and planning, particularly on instances larger than those they were trained on, impacting their trustworthiness in high-risk applications.

10. AI agents show early promise. The 2024 launch of RE-Bench introduced a rigorous benchmark for AI agents. In short time-horizon settings with a two-hour budget, top AI systems score four times higher than human experts; however, in 32-hour budget settings, humans outscore AI two to one. AI agents already match human expertise in specific tasks, such as writing Triton kernels, while operating faster and at lower costs.
