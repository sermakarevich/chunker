# math-reasoning-and-general-reasoning-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities|ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities]] — Evaluation frameworks across embeddings, long-context, vision, video, speech, coding, and mathematics show rapid saturation and paradigm shifts, with benchmarks like MTEB, RULER, and SWE-bench tracking models like voyage-3-m-exp (74.03), Gemini-1.5-Pro (95.5), and OpenAI’s o3 (71.7% on SWE-bench Verified, 87.7% on GPQA Diamond, and 25.2% on FrontierMath). Progress in theorem proving and general reasoning is similarly documented through AlphaProof's Olympiad performance, TPTP's 89% automation rate, and MMMU's 78.2% score for o1.

The AI Index Report 2025 Chapter 2 evaluates technical performance through standardized benchmarks focusing on mathematical reasoning and general AI capabilities.

**Mathematics Evaluation**
GSM8K, introduced by OpenAI in 2021, contains approximately 8,000 grade-school math word problems and challenges AI models to generate multistep solutions using arithmetic operations. Alongside MMLU, it is a widely used benchmark for evaluating advanced large language models, though concerns regarding potential contamination and benchmark saturation have emerged. The top-performing model on GSM8K is a variant of Claude Sonnet 3.5 optimized using the HPT prompting strategy, which achieved a 97.72% accuracy score. This represents a significant improvement over the previous high of 91.00% in 2023. However, in 2024, multiple models from Mistral, Meta, and Qwen scored around 96%, indicating that the GSM8K benchmark may be approaching saturation.

The MATH dataset, introduced in 2021 by researchers from UC Berkeley and the University of Chicago, contains 12,500 challenging, competition-level mathematics problems. AI systems initially solved only 6.9% of these problems when the benchmark was first released. Performance improved dramatically, and in January 2025, OpenAI’s o3-mini (high) model solved 97.9% of the problems, surpassing the 90% human baseline for the first time.

The LMSYS Chatbot Arena includes a dedicated math filter that evaluates over 181 models based on public preference for math-related answers, accumulating more than 340,000 public votes. Unlike the coding arena where Gemini-based models lead, the top-ranked model in the Math Arena is OpenAI’s o1 variant, which was released in December 2024.

To address the saturation of high school and college-level benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH, Epoch AI introduced FrontierMath. This benchmark features hundreds of original, exceptionally challenging problems vetted by expert mathematicians, often requiring hours, days, or collaborative research efforts to solve. The problems test advanced mathematics including number theory, real analysis, algebraic geometry, and category theory. When evaluated on FrontierMath, Gemini 1.5 Pro achieved the highest score of 2.0% among six leading LLMs (o1-preview, o1-mini, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Grok 2 Beta, and Gemini 1.5 Pro 002). OpenAI’s o3 model scored 25.2% on the benchmark.

**Theorem Proving and Mathematical Systems**
DeepMind developed AlphaProof, a reinforcement learning system derived from AlphaZero, to solve mathematical problems by generating hypotheses that are verified using the Lean interactive proof system. A fine-tuned Gemini model translates natural language problem statements into formal representations to build a comprehensive training library. AlphaGeometry 2 is a neuro-symbolic hybrid system featuring a language model based on Gemini and trained on extensive synthetic data. Prior to 2024, AlphaGeometry solved 83% of historical International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) geometry problems. The IMO, established in 1959, is the world’s oldest and most prestigious competition for young mathematicians. During the 2024 competition, AlphaGeometry solved the sole geometry problem in just 24 seconds, while AlphaProof solved 25 out of 30 Olympiad geometry problems, surpassing the average score of an IMO silver medalist (22.9). AlphaProof also successfully solved two algebra problems and one number theory problem but failed to solve two combinatorics problems.

Automatic theorem-proving systems are also evaluated on the TPTP benchmark, which has been used since 1997 to assess performance on software verification and logic problems. A 2024 update of TPTP v.9.0.0, containing over 25,000 problems, indicates that fully automatic systems can now solve 89% of its problems. Cross-testing these systems remains unfeasible due to differing logical frameworks and a lack of translators between them, while the TPTP library size is insufficient to serve as a training set for AlphaProof without generating substantial synthetic examples.

**General Reasoning Benchmarks**
General reasoning evaluates AI systems' ability to draw logically valid conclusions across broad, multidisciplinary domains rather than narrow tasks. The MMMU benchmark, developed by researchers from the United States and Canada, assesses this using approximately 11,500 college-level questions across six core disciplines: art and design, business, science, health and medicine, humanities and social science, and technology and engineering. Questions utilize formats including charts, maps, tables, and chemical structures. As of January 2025, OpenAI’s o1 model achieved a 78.2% score on MMMU, a significant improvement from the 59.4% state-of-the-art score reported in the previous year. This score remains below the 82.6% medium human expert baseline, though AI systems are rapidly closing the gap.

The GPQA benchmark, introduced in 2023 by researchers from NYU, Anthropic, and Meta, tests graduate-level general and multisubject reasoning using 448 difficult multiple-choice questions crafted by subject-matter experts in biology, physics, and chemistry. These questions cannot be answered by web search. On the dataset's most challenging "diamond" subset, human experts achieved an 81.3% accuracy rate. OpenAI’s o3 model, launched in December 2024, scored 87.7%, exceeding the human expert baseline and marking a 48.9 percentage point improvement over 2023's state-of-the-art.

The ARC-AGI benchmark addresses claims regarding artificial general intelligence by testing a system's ability to generalize beyond prior training. It presents AI systems with a set of independent tasks, each containing demonstration or input pairs followed by test or output scenarios, rather than relying on memorization or narrow pattern matching.
