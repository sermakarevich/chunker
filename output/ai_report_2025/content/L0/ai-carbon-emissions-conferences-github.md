# ai-carbon-emissions-conferences-github

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-report-2025-training-costs-environment-community|ai-index-report-2025-training-costs-environment-community]] — The AI Index Report 2025 details the rise in AI training costs—with Llama 3.1-405B costing an estimated $170 million—and carbon emissions, which rose from 0.01 tons for AlexNet to 8,930 tons for Llama 3.1-405B. It also highlights the growth of the AI research community, including NeurIPS attracting nearly 20,000 participants in 2024, and AI-related GitHub projects growing to 4.3 million by 2024.

The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, reports that carbon emissions from training frontier AI models have steadily increased over time. While the emissions from training AlexNet (2012) were negligible (0.01 tons of CO2 equivalent), GPT-3 (released in 2020) reportedly emitted around 588 tons of carbon, GPT-4 (2023) emitted 5,184 tons, and Llama 3.1 405B (2024) emitted 8,930 tons. DeepSeek V3, released in 2024 and with performance comparable to OpenAI’s o1, is estimated to have carbon emissions comparable to GPT-3, which was released five years prior. For context, the average American emits 18.08 tons of carbon per capita per year.

To estimate these emissions, the AI Index used carbon data published by model developers and supplemented it with calculations from a widely used online AI training emissions calculator. This supplementation was necessary because many developers do not disclose their models’ carbon footprints. The calculator estimates emissions based on the type of hardware used for training, total training hours, cloud provider, and training region. The AI Index sourced input data—such as training hardware and duration—for the emissions calculator from various online sources and validated the accuracy of the calculator by comparing its estimates with actual emissions reported by developers, finding the results were largely consistent.

Additionally, the report provides data on other carbon-emitting activities: air travel for one passenger from New York to San Francisco emits 0.99 tons, the average human life emits 5.51 tons per year, and average car usage (including fuel) over a lifetime emits 63 tons.

Beyond environmental impact, the AI Index Report 2025 analyzes AI research and professional networking. AI conferences have expanded in scale, quantity, and prestige over the past two decades. Attendance at major AI conferences grew almost 21.7% from 2023 to 2024, with total attendees rising by more than 60,000 since 2014. Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS) remains the most attended AI conference, attracting almost 20,000 participants in 2024. Other major conferences that saw attendance increases in the last year include CVPR, ICML, ICRA, ICLR, IROS, and AAAI. In 2021, a significant spike in ICML attendance was attributed to the conference being held virtually. Total attendance figures encompass virtual, hybrid, and in-person participation.

Furthermore, the report examines open-source AI software via GitHub data. Since 2011, the number of AI-related GitHub projects has consistently increased, growing from 1,549 in 2011 to approximately 4.3 million in 2024, with a sharp 40.3% rise in the last year alone. As of 2024, the United States accounts for 23.4% of GitHub AI project contributions, followed by India at 19.9% and Europe at 19.5%, though the U.S. share has declined since 2016. China accounts for 2.08% of contributions. The total number of new GitHub stars for AI-related projects rose from 14.0 million in 2023 to 17.7 million in 2024. In 2024, the United States led in cumulative GitHub stars with 21.08 million, followed by the 'Rest of the world' (16.39 million), Europe (10.29 million), India (4.06 million), and China (3.67 million). Popular libraries such as TensorFlow, Keras, PyTorch, and OpenCV are among the most starred repositories.
