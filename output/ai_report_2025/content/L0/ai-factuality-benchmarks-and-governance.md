# ai-factuality-benchmarks-and-governance

**Parent:** [[content/L1/responsible-ai-factuality-benchmarks|responsible-ai-factuality-benchmarks]] — The AI Index Report 2025 highlights a record high of 233 AI-related incidents in 2024, alongside the development of new factuality benchmarks like FACTS Grounding (led by Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp at 83.6%) and SimpleQA (led by o1-preview at 42.7%). It also defines six key dimensions of Responsible AI and details a McKinsey survey showing 21% of business leaders assign AI governance oversight to information security departments.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, discusses new benchmarks introduced in 2024 to evaluate the factuality of Large Language Models (LLMs).

One such benchmark is the Hughes Hallucination Evaluation Model (HHEM) leaderboard. However, the report notes that the HHEM leaderboard appears to be nearing saturation as model performance improves, and its focus on news articles and summarization tasks limits its comprehensiveness. This has created a need for benchmarks that assess factuality in more challenging and diverse contexts.

To address this, Google introduced the FACTS Grounding benchmark. FACTS assesses how well LLMs form responses to user requests based on context documents covering diverse domains, including finance, technology, retail, medicine, and law. To provide satisfactory answers, models must craft long-form responses that are both factually accurate and detailed enough. FACTS is more complex than HHEM, as it requires models to perform tasks such as summarization, question-and-answer generation, fact-finding, and explanation. Responses are evaluated by a collection of AI models—Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet—which assign a factuality score. Currently, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp holds the highest grounding score at 83.6%.

Additionally, OpenAI researchers introduced SimpleQA to address the challenge of evaluating long AI answers that contain multiple factual claims. SimpleQA presents models with over 4,000 short, straightforward, easily gradable, and relatively challenging fact-seeking questions spanning topics such as history, science and technology, art, and geography. SimpleQA represents a significant challenge for leading LLMs; the best-performing model, OpenAI's o1-preview, successfully answers only 42.7% of the questions. Among models that attempted to respond, o1-preview scored 47.0% of "correct-given-attempted" prompts, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 44.5%. The report notes that larger models tend to perform better on SimpleQA, though some models, such as the Claude-3 family, refrained from responding to 75% of the prompts.

Furthermore, the AI Index partnered with McKinsey & Company in 2024 to survey 759 business leaders from over 30 countries regarding the integration of Responsible AI (RAI) into their operations. The survey defined RAI as a framework for ensuring that AI is developed and deployed in a safe, trustworthy, and ethical manner, assessing it across the dimensions of privacy and data governance, fairness, transparency and explainability, and security and safety. The survey found that no single department dominates AI governance; the most common department with primary oversight is information security (cyber/fraud/privacy) at 21%, followed by data and analytics at 17%. Additionally, 14% of respondents reported having dedicated AI governance roles.
