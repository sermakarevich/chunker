# vision-video-speech-coding-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025|ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025]] — The AI Index Report 2025 highlights significant leaps in AI reasoning (OpenAI o1/o3), long-context retrieval (Gemini 1.5 Pro), and coding (o3 solving 71.7% of SWE-bench), while noting the saturation of early benchmarks like HumanEval.

The AI community has increasingly used public evaluation platforms like the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard to assess the capabilities of leading AI systems, including top AI image generators. The Chatbot Arena Leaderboard features a Vision Arena that ranks the performance of over 50 vision models. In this arena, users submit text-to-image prompts—for example, "Batman drinking a coffee"—and vote for their preferred generation. To date, the Vision Arena has gathered more than 150,000 votes. As of early 2025, the top-ranked vision model on the Chatbot Arena leaderboard is Google’s Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking-Exp-1219. Performance among leading models is closely clustered; for instance, the gap between the top-ranked model and the fourth-ranked model, ChatGPT-4o-latest (2024-1120), is only 3.4%.

Regarding video generation, recent years have seen the rise of models capable of creating videos from text prompts. While earlier models had limitations such as low quality, lack of sound, or very short clips, 2024 marked a significant leap forward. In November 2023, Stability AI launched Stable Video Diffusion, its first foundation model for high-quality video generation. In March 2024, Stability AI introduced Stable Video 3D, which generates multiple 3D views and videos of an object from a single image. In February 2024, OpenAI previewed Sora, a diffusion model that creates a base video and refines it by removing noise over multiple steps; Sora became publicly accessible in December 2024 and can generate 20-second videos at resolutions up to 1080p. In October 2024, Meta unveiled the latest version of Movie Gen, which includes personalized video generation from images, high-quality video editing based on instructions, and the ability to incorporate sound; Movie Gen can create 16-second videos at 1080p resolution and 16 frames per second. Google also launched two video generation models: Veo in May 2024 and Veo 2 in December 2024. Internal benchmarking by Google showed that Veo 2 outperformed other leading generators including Meta’s Movie Gen, Kling v1.5, and Sora Turbo, with users consistently favoring Veo 2 in comparisons. Other notable contributions include Runway’s Gen-3 Alpha, Luma’s Dream Machine, and Kuaishou’s Kling 1.5. Progress is illustrated by the "Will Smith eating spaghetti" prompt, where videos generated by the generator Pika in 2025 show dramatic quality improvements over those produced in 2023.

In the domain of speech, AI systems can transcribe spoken words to text and recognize individual speakers. Speech recognition, the ability to convert spoken words into text, is evaluated on the LRS2 (Oxford-BBC Lip Reading Sentences 2) benchmark, introduced in 2017, which uses audio-visual clips from talk shows and news programs. Performance is measured by Word Error Rate (WER), where lower scores indicate higher precision. The model Whisper-Flamingo set a new standard on the LRS2 benchmark with a WER of 1.3%, surpassing the 2023 state-of-the-art score. However, the AI Index Report 2025 suggests this benchmark may be nearing saturation.

In the domain of coding, Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used as assistants. Because foundational benchmarks like HumanEval—which uses 164 handwritten programming problems—are becoming saturated (with Claude 3.5 Sonnet (HPT) achieving a score of 100%), researchers are moving toward more complex challenges. SWE-bench, introduced in October 2023 by Princeton and University of Chicago researchers, uses 2,294 software engineering problems from GitHub and Python repositories. On the SWE-bench Verified subset, the best performing model at the end of 2023 scored 4.4%, but by early 2025, OpenAI’s o3 model solved 71.7% of the problems. To address the need for tests that require invoking diverse functions across libraries, an international team released BigCodeBench in 2024. BigCodeBench consists of 1,140 tasks across 139 libraries and seven domains. On the hard subset of BigCodeBench (specifically the "complete" and "instruct" tasks), the best model, OpenAI’s o1, achieved an average score of 35.5. On the Chatbot Arena LLM leaderboard's coding filter, the top model is Gemini-Exp-1206 with a score of 1,369, followed by OpenAI’s o1 at 1,361.
