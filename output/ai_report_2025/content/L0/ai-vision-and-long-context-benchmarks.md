# ai-vision-and-long-context-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025|ai-technical-performance-benchmarks-2025]] — The AI Index Report 2025 highlights significant leaps in AI reasoning (OpenAI o1/o3), long-context retrieval (Gemini 1.5 Pro), and coding (o3 solving 71.7% of SWE-bench), while noting the saturation of early benchmarks like HumanEval.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, provides an analysis of language embedding and long-context retrieval capabilities, as well as computer vision and image generation.

Regarding the Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB), which was introduced in late 2022 and covers eight tasks (bitext mining, classification, clustering, pair classification, reranking, retrieval, semantic textual similarity, and summarization), the top-performing embedding model as of early 2025 was Voyage AI’s voyage-3-m-exp with a score of 74.03. This model, a variant of the voyage-3-large foundation model designed for embedding tasks, utilizes quantization-aware training and Matryoshka Representation Learning to improve performance. Voyage AI specializes in high-quality AI embedding models. The voyage-3-m-exp model narrowly outperformed NV-Embed-v2, which had a score of 72.31 and held the top spot for most of 2024. Since the introduction of MTEB in late 2022, when the leading model scored 59.5, average performance on the benchmark has meaningfully improved.

As the context windows of Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded, evaluating long-context settings has become more critical. Early evaluations typically focused on "needle-in-the-haystack" scenarios, where models retrieve specific pieces of information from lengthy texts. However, more recent models, such as GPT-4o (May 2024) and Gemini 2.0 Pro Experimental (February 2025), now boast context windows ranging from 128 thousand to 2 million tokens. In contrast, 2023 models like GPT-4 and Llama 2, released by OpenAI and Meta respectively, featured windows of 8,000 and 4,000 tokens. 

To address limitations in long-context assessments, several new evaluation suites were introduced in 2024. Nvidia’s RULER benchmark assesses long-context performance through multihop reasoning, aggregation, retrieval performance, and question answering. On RULER, Gemini-1.5-Pro achieved the highest weighted performance average of 95.5, followed by GPT-4 (89.0) and GLM4 (88.0). The RULER team also found that while most popular LLMs claim context sizes of 32K tokens or greater, only half can maintain satisfactory performance at that length, meaning actual operational context windows are often shorter than claimed.

Another long-context evaluation benchmark introduced in 2024 is HELMET (How to Evaluate Long-Context Models Effectively and Thoroughly), a collaboration between Intel and Princeton. HELMET features seven evaluation categories, including passage re-ranking and synthetic recall. On the HELMET benchmark across 8K, 32K, and 128K context settings, models such as Gemini 1.5 Pro and the August variant of GPT-4 maintained their effectiveness, while GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Llama 3.1-70B showed performance degradation in longer settings.

In the domain of computer vision, vision models are evaluated on their ability to understand images and videos. While ImageNet was a foundational benchmark for straightforward tasks like image classification, modern benchmarks focus on more complex, open-ended challenges. The Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) challenge, introduced in 2019 by the University of Washington and the Allen Institute for AI, tests the ability of AI systems to answer questions and provide the rationale behind those answers, measured by the Q->AR score. In July 2024, a model reached a Q->AR score of 85.0, matching the human benchmark and representing a 4.2% improvement over 2023.

For video understanding, the MVBench benchmark, introduced in 2023 by researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai AI Laboratory, the University of Hong Kong, Fudan University, and Nanjing University, requires temporal reasoning across multiple frames. As of 2024, the top model on MVBench is Video-CCAM-7B-v1.2 (built on the Qwen 2.5-7B-Instruct language model) with a score of 69.23, a 14.6% improvement since the benchmark's introduction in late 2023.

Regarding image generation, the report notes that AI-generated images of human faces are now frequently indistinguishable from real photos. Midjourney's progress is exemplified by its evolution from 2022 to 2025; while its 2022 models produced cartoonish renderings of "a hyper-realistic image of Harry Potter," the 2025 versions create startlingly realistic depictions.
