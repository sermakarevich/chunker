# ai-index-2025-economy-science-policy-education-opinion

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis|ai-index-report-2025-comprehensive-analysis]] — The Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025 by Stanford HAI details a record $252.3 billion in global private AI investment and a 280-fold decrease in GPT-3.5 level inference costs, while noting that the top two frontier AI models now differ by only 0.7% in performance.

According to the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, the following highlights are provided across economy, science, medicine, policy, education, and public opinion.

### Chapter 4: Economy (continued)

Generative AI now represents more than 20% of all AI-related private investment. In terms of global AI private investment, the United States widened its lead in 2024, reaching $109.1 billion, which is nearly 12 times higher than China’s $9.3 billion and 24 times the U.K.’s $4.5 billion. The investment gap is more pronounced in generative AI, where U.S. investment exceeded the combined total of China, the European Union, and the U.K. by $25.4 billion in 2024, expanding on a $21.8 billion gap in 2023.

Organizational use of AI reached unprecedented levels in 2024, with the proportion of survey respondents reporting AI use by their organizations jumping from 55% in 2023 to 78% in 2024. Additionally, the proportion of respondents reporting the use of generative AI in at least one business function more than doubled, rising from 33% in 2023 to 71% in 2024.

AI is beginning to deliver financial impact across business functions, although most companies are in the early stages. Among companies reporting financial impacts from AI within a business function, 49% of respondents using AI in service operations, 43% in supply chain management, and 41% in software engineering report cost savings, though most report these savings are less than 10%. Regarding revenue, 71% of respondents using AI in marketing and sales, 63% in supply chain management, and 57% in service operations report revenue gains, but the most common level of increase is less than 5%.

Regional shifts in AI use are evident, with Greater China showing significant growth. While North America maintains leadership in organizational AI use, Greater China had one of the most significant year-over-year growth rates with a 27 percentage point increase. Europe followed with a 23 percentage point increase.

China’s dominance in industrial robotics continues. In 2023, China installed 276,300 industrial robots, which is six times more than Japan and 7.3 times more than the United States. Since surpassing Japan in 2013 (when China accounted for 20.8% of global installations), China's share of global installations has risen to 51.1%. Although China continues to install more robots than the rest of the world combined, the margin narrowed slightly in 2023.

Collaborative and interactive robot installations are becoming more common. Collaborative robots grew from 2.8% of all new industrial robot installations in 2017 to 10.5% by 2023. In 2023, there was also a rise in service robot installations across all application categories except medical robotics.

AI is driving interest in nuclear energy to power operations. Microsoft announced a $1.6 billion deal to revive the Three Mile Island nuclear reactor, and Google and Amazon have also secured nuclear energy agreements to support AI operations.

Research confirms that AI boosts productivity and helps narrow the gap between low- and high-skilled workers.

### Chapter 5: Science and Medicine

In 2024, large-scale, high-performance protein sequencing models such as ESM3 and AlphaFold 3 were launched, continuing a trend of increasing model size and improved protein prediction accuracy.

AI continues to drive advances in scientific discovery, including the launch of Aviary, which trains LLM agents for biological tasks, and FireSat, which enhances wildfire prediction.

Clinical knowledge in leading LLMs is improving. OpenAI’s o1 model achieved a state-of-the-art 96.0% on the MedQA benchmark, a 5.8 percentage point gain over the best 2023 score. Since late 2022, performance on MedQA has improved by 28.4 percentage points.

AI has shown the ability to outperform doctors in specific clinical tasks. A study found GPT-4 outperformed doctors (both with and without AI) in diagnosing complex clinical cases, and other studies show AI surpassing doctors in cancer detection and identifying high-mortality-risk patients. However, research suggests that AI-doctor collaboration may yield the best results.

FDA-approved, AI-enabled medical devices have increased significantly; only six were approved by 2015, but the number rose to 223 by 2023.

Synthetic data shows promise in medicine for identifying social determinants of health, enhancing privacy-preserving clinical risk prediction, and discovering new drug compounds.

Publications on medical AI ethics grew from 288 in 2020 to 1,031 in 2024.

In 2024, medical foundation models were released, including general-purpose multimodal models like Med-Gemini and specialized models such as EchoCLIP (echocardiology), VisionFM (ophthalmology), and ChexAgent (radiology).

Public protein science databases have grown since 2021, with entries increasing in UniProt (31%), PDB (23%), and AlphaFold (585%).

AI-driven research received two Nobel Prizes in 2024: Demis Hassabis and John Jumper (Google DeepMind) won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for protein folding with AlphaFold, and John Hopfield and Geoffrey Hinton won the Nobel Prize in Physics for their foundational contributions to neural networks.

### Chapter 6: Policy and Governance

U.S. states are leading AI legislation due to slow federal progress. State-level AI laws increased from one in 2016 to 49 by 2023, and to 131 in 2024.

Global governments are investing in AI infrastructure: Canada has a $2.4 billion package, China has a $47.5 billion semiconductor fund, France committed $117 billion, India pledged $1.25 billion, and Saudi Arabia’s Project Transcendence includes a $100 billion investment.

Mentions of AI in legislative proceedings across 75 countries increased by 21.3% in 2024, rising to 1,889 from 1,557 in 2023. Since 2016, total mentions have grown more than ninefold.

International AI safety institutes were launched in 2024. The first emerged in November 2023 in the U.S. and U.K. Following the AI Seoul Summit in May 2024, additional institutes were pledged in Japan, France, Germany, Italy, Singapore, South Korea, Australia, Canada, and the European Union.

In 2024, the U.S. introduced 59 AI-related federal regulations from 42 unique agencies, more than double the 25 regulations from 21 agencies in 2023.

U.S. states have expanded deepfake regulations; by 2024, 24 states had passed regulations targeting deepfakes, with 15 more states (including Oregon, New Mexico, and New York) introducing measures in 2024.

### Chapter 7: Education

In the U.S., access to high school computer science (CS) courses has increased slightly, though gaps remain across demographics. While 81% of U.S. high school CS teachers agree that AI should be part of a foundational CS experience, fewer than half feel equipped to teach it.

Two-thirds of countries worldwide now offer or plan to offer K–12 CS education, a figure that has doubled since 2019.

In the U.S., the number of graduates earning master’s degrees in AI nearly doubled between 2022 and 2023.

The U.S. is a global leader in producing information, technology, and communications (ICT) graduates, followed by Spain, Brazil, and the United Kingdom.

### Chapter 8: Public Opinion

Global optimism about AI products and services has risen, with the share of individuals who see them as more beneficial than harmful increasing from 52% in 2022 to 55% in 2024. Among 26 nations surveyed by Ipsos, 18 saw an increase in optimism.

Two-thirds of people globally believe that AI-powered products and services will significantly impact daily life within the next three to five years, a 6 percentage point increase since 2022. The largest jumps in perception were in Canada (17%) and Germany (15%).

Trust in AI companies is declining. Global confidence that AI companies protect personal data fell from 50% in 2023 to 47% in 2024, and fewer people believe AI systems are unbiased and free from discrimination.

Regional differences in AI optimism persist. A majority of people in China (83%), Indonesia (80%), and and Thailand (77%) view AI as beneficial, while a minority in Canada (40%), United States (39%), and the Netherlands (36%) share this view.

In the U.S., 61% of people fear self-driving cars, and only 13% trust them, according to an American Automobile Association survey.

Local U.S. policymakers support AI regulation; in 2023, 73.7% of local policymakers agreed that AI should be regulated, up from 55.7% in 2022. Support was stronger among Democrats (79.2%) than Republicans (55.5%).

Countries previously most skeptical of AI showed the sharpest gains in optimism. From 2022 to 2024, optimism rose by 8% in the US, 10% in Germany, 8% in Canada, 10% in France, and 8% in Great Britain.

Workers expect AI to reshape jobs, but fear of replacement remains lower.
