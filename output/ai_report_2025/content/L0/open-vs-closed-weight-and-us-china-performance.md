# open-vs-closed-weight-and-us-china-performance

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-technical-performance-2024-2025|ai-technical-performance-2024-2025]] — The Technical Performance chapter of the AI Index Report 2025 details how AI models in 2024 achieved unprecedented benchmark gains (e.g., SWE-bench rising from 4.4% to 71.7%) and closed the gap between open-weight and closed-weight models, as well as US and Chinese models (e.g., MMLU gap narrowing from 17.5 to 0.3 percentage points). It also highlights the lauch of advanced models like OpenAI's o1 and o3, and the lauch of Meta's Llama 3.1 405B.

The Technical Performance chapter of the Artificial Intelligence Index Report 2025, published by the AI Index Steering Committee at Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered AI, analyzes the performance gap between closed-weight and open-weight AI models. In 2023, closed-weight models consistently outperformed open-weight counterparts on nearly every major benchmark, including MMLU, HumanEval, MMMU, and MATH. By 2024, however, this gap narrowed significantly. For example, in late 2023, closed-weight models led open models on MMLU by 15.9 percentage points, but by the end of 2024, this difference shrunk to 0.1 percentage point. This rapid improvement in open-weight performance was largely driven by Meta's summer release of Llama 3.1, followed by other high-performing open-weight models such as DeepSeek's V3. Similarly, on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena, the leading closed-weight model outperformed the top open-weight model by 8.0% in early January 2024, narrowing to a 1.7% gap by February 2025.

AI models are released with different levels of openness. Some models, such as Google's Med-Gemini, remain entirely closed and accessible only to their developers. Others, like OpenAI's GPT-4o and Anthropic's Claude 3.5, provide limited public access through APIs, but their weights are not released, preventing independent modification or public scrutiny. In contrast, weights for Meta's Llama 3.3 and Stable Video 4D are fully available, allowing anyone to modify and use them freely. The AI Index notes that while open-weight models are more accessible than closed-weight models, they are not necessarily fully 'open source' because the underlying code or training data is often withheld. Perspectives on this divide are split: advocates argue open-weight models reduce monopolies, spur innovation, and improve transparency, citing the use of Meta's Llama models in creating tools like Meditron and powering military applications. Critics, however, warn that open-weight models pose security risks, including the potential for creating bioweapons or spreading disinformation.

Furthermore, the report examines the technical performance gap between the United States and China. Historically, the U.S. has dominated AI research and model development, but China-based models are rapidly catching up. In January 2024, the top U.S. model outperformed the best Chinese model by 9.3% on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena, but by February 2025, this gap narrowed to 1.7%. At the end of 2023, performance gaps between U.S. and Chinese models on the MMLU, MMMU, MATH, and HumanEval benchmarks were 17.5, 13.5, 24.3, and 31.6 percentage points, respectively. By the end of 2024, these differences narrowed to 0.3, 8.1, 1.6, and 3.7 percentage points. The launch of DeepSeek-R1 was particularly notable because the company reported achieving these results using only a fraction of the hardware resources typically required for such models, raising doubts about the effectiveness of U.S. semiconductor export controls.

Additionally, the report highlights the rise of smaller, high-performing AI models. While AI progress has traditionally been driven by scaling (increasing model size and data), 2024 saw a breakthrough in compact models, including GPT-4o mini, o1-mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Llama 3.1 8B, and Mistral Small 3.5. Smaller models are significant because they demonstrate increased algorithmic efficiency, lower training costs, and faster, less expensive inference. This trend is exemplified by the reduction in the size of the smallest model scoring above 60% on the MMLU benchmark: in 2022, the smallest such model was PaLM with 540 billion parameters, but by 2024, Microsoft's Phi-3 Mini achieved this threshold with only 3.8 billion parameters, a 142-fold reduction in size.

Finally, the report discusses the convergence of performance at the frontier. The AI landscape is becoming more competitive, with multiple providers now offering highly capable models. The Elo score difference between the top and 10th-ranked models on the Chatbot Arena Leaderboard narrowed from 11.9% (as reported in the previous AI Index) to 5.4% by early 2025. The difference between the top two models shrank from 4.9% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024. Current top providers include Google, OpenAI, DeepSeek, xAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Mistral AI. The report also notes that many prominent AI benchmarks are reaching saturation, as AI systems advance so rapidly that new tests remain relevant for only a few years. To address this, the community is moving toward more rigorous evaluations, as some developers have used nonstandard prompting (such as Google's use of chain-of-thought prompting for Gemini Ultra's MMLU score) or reported results that perform worse in independent third-party testing.
