# ai_professional_transitions_methodology

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-workforce-methodology-and-metrics|ai-workforce-methodology-and-metrics]] — This block details the methodology for analyzing AI labor market trends using LinkedIn data and the 'Worldized' dataset, distinguishing between 'AI skills' and the broader 'AI-related skills' taxonomy to track skill penetration, AI-powered roles, and the transition of software engineers and data scientists into AI-centric work.

This document details the methodology and data sources used to analyze AI-related trends, specifically focusing on the LinkedIn-based data used to track professional shifts and the technical parameters of the AI tools themselves. In the context of professional transitions, it highlights that a significant portion of the workforce is moving toward AI-centric roles. Specifically, it discusses the 'AI-related' roles and the 'non-AI' roles, noting that the transition is most prevalent among software engineers and data scientists. The data reveals that the demand for AI skills has outpaced the supply, leading to a surge in certifications and self-taught learning. Furthermore, the document outlines the technical specifications of the models used for these analyses, including the training data’s cutoff dates and the specific architectural iterations of the LLMs employed to categorize job descriptions into AI and non-AI categories.
