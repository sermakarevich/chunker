# text-embedding-long-context-and-visual-reasoning-benchmarks

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities|ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities]] — Evaluation frameworks across embeddings, long-context, vision, video, speech, coding, and mathematics show rapid saturation and paradigm shifts, with benchmarks like MTEB, RULER, and SWE-bench tracking models like voyage-3-m-exp (74.03), Gemini-1.5-Pro (95.5), and OpenAI’s o3 (71.7% on SWE-bench Verified, 87.7% on GPQA Diamond, and 25.2% on FrontierMath). Progress in theorem proving and general reasoning is similarly documented through AlphaProof's Olympiad performance, TPTP's 89% automation rate, and MMMU's 78.2% score for o1.

The Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB), developed by a team at Hugging Face and Cohere and introduced in late 2022, comprehensively evaluates how models perform on embedding tasks. Embedding converts data, such as words, texts, or documents, into numerical vectors that capture semantic meanings and distances between vectors. This transformation process is essential for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), as it enables models to search for relevant information after transforming user queries into embedding vectors. The MTEB includes 58 datasets spanning 112 languages and eight embedding tasks: bitext mining, classification, clustering, pair classification, reranking, retrieval, semantic textual similarity, and summarization. In bitext mining, the model matches sentences across two different languages. As of early 2025, Voyage AI’s voyage-3-m-exp leads the MTEB leaderboard with a score of 74.03. Voyage AI specializes in creating high-quality AI embedding models. The voyage-3-m-exp model is a variant of the voyage-3-large foundation model designed specifically for embedding tasks, and it utilizes strategies like Matryoshka Representation Learning and quantization-aware training. This model narrowly outperformed NV-Embed-v2, which scored 72.31 and held the top position for most of 2024. When the MTEB benchmark launched in late 2022, the leading model achieved an average score of 59.5, demonstrating meaningful performance improvements over the subsequent two years.

As large language models expanded their context windows from 4,000 to 8,000 tokens (as seen with 2023 models like GPT-4 and Llama 2) to 128,000 to 2 million tokens (as seen with May 2024 models like GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Pro Experimental), evaluating their performance in long-context settings became increasingly critical. Existing evaluations previously relied on limited needle-in-the-haystack scenarios that only provided baseline assessments. To address this limitation, Nvidia introduced the RULER benchmark in 2024 to assess long-context performance through retrieval, multihop reasoning, aggregation, and question answering. According to February 2025 results, Gemini-1.5-Pro achieved the highest weighted performance average of 95.5 on RULER, followed by GPT-4 at 89.0 and GLM4 at 88.0. The RULER researchers discovered that many models claiming context sizes of 32,000 tokens or greater fail to maintain satisfactory performance at that length, revealing that their actual operational context windows are shorter than advertised. Additionally, HELMET, a long-context evaluation benchmark introduced in 2024 through a collaboration between Intel and Princeton University, targets the inadequacies of prior benchmarks, including insufficient downstream task coverage, short context lengths, and unreliable metrics. HELMET features seven long-context evaluation categories and tests models across 8,000, 32,000, and 128,000 context settings. On the HELMET benchmark, models including GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Llama 3.1-70B experience performance degradation in longer context settings, while Gemini 1.5 Pro and the August variant of GPT-4 maintain their effectiveness.

In image and video understanding, computer vision allows machines to comprehend and create visuals. While early deep learning relied on ImageNet for image understanding, modern benchmarks test more complex reasoning. The Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) challenge, introduced in 2019 by researchers from the University of Washington and the Allen Institute for AI, tests AI systems' commonsense visual reasoning abilities by requiring them to answer questions based on images and reason about the logic behind those answers. Performance is measured using the Q->AR score, which evaluates both question answering and rationale selection. The human baseline for VCR is a score of 85.0. In July 2024, a model posted to the VCR leaderboard achieved a score of 85.0, matching the human baseline and marking a 4.2% improvement since 2023. Another benchmark, MVBench, was introduced in late 2023 by researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai AI Laboratory, University of Hong Kong, Fudan University, and Nanjing University. MVBench tests multimodal video understanding and temporal reasoning across multiple frames, unlike earlier benchmarks that focused on spatial understanding through static images. As of 2024, the top MVBench model is Video-CCAM-7B-v1.2, built on the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct language model, which achieved a score of 69.23. This score represents a 14.6% improvement on the benchmark since its late 2023 introduction. Regarding image generation, current AI generators are so advanced that most people struggle to differentiate between AI-generated images and actual images of human faces.
