# image-video-speech-coding-benchmarks-2025

**Parent:** [[content/L1/ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities|ai-evaluation-benchmarks-across-modalities]] — Evaluation frameworks across embeddings, long-context, vision, video, speech, coding, and mathematics show rapid saturation and paradigm shifts, with benchmarks like MTEB, RULER, and SWE-bench tracking models like voyage-3-m-exp (74.03), Gemini-1.5-Pro (95.5), and OpenAI’s o3 (71.7% on SWE-bench Verified, 87.7% on GPQA Diamond, and 25.2% on FrontierMath). Progress in theorem proving and general reasoning is similarly documented through AlphaProof's Olympiad performance, TPTP's 89% automation rate, and MMMU's 78.2% score for o1.

This section details progress in image generation, video synthesis, speech recognition, and automated coding benchmarks. Midjourney’s image generation models from 2022 to 2025 demonstrate significant improvements in hyper-realism using the prompt “a hyper-realistic image of Harry Potter.” In 2022, early versions (V1 through V4) produced cartoonish and inaccurate renderings, but by 2025, later versions (V6.1) generate startlingly realistic depictions. This evolution occurred alongside the growth of the Chatbot Arena Vision leaderboard, which ranks over 50 vision models based on user-submitted text-to-image prompts like “Batman drinking a coffee.” By early 2025, Google’s Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking-Exp-1219 leads the Vision Arena with more than 150,000 user votes. The performance gap between the top model and the fourth-ranked model, ChatGPT-4o-latest (released November 20, 2024), is only 3.4%.

The AI industry experienced rapid advances in text-to-video generation in 2024, building on earlier models that struggled with low quality, lack of sound, and short clip lengths. In November 2023, Stability AI launched Stable Video Diffusion, its first foundation model for high-quality video generation. Stability AI subsequently released Stable Video 3D in March 2024 to generate 3D views and videos from single images. In March 2024, OpenAI previewed Sora, a diffusion-based video generation model that creates 20-second clips at up to 1080p resolution by progressively refining a base video; it became publicly accessible in December 2024. Meta introduced its advanced Movie Gen model in October 2024, which supports instruction-based video editing, sound incorporation, and 16-second 1080p videos at 16 frames per second. Google launched Veo in May 2024 and Veo 2 in December 2024; internal benchmarks show Veo 2 consistently outperformed Movie Gen, Kling v1.5, and Sora Turbo in user preference tests. Other contributors include Runway’s Gen-3 Alpha, Luma’s Dream Machine, and Kuaishou’s Kling 1.5. The trajectory of improvement is visible in Pika’s generations of the prompt “Will Smith eating spaghetti,” which showed dramatic quality gains between versions released in December 2023 (V1.0) and February 2025 (V2.2).

AI speech recognition has matured to the point where transcription is highly reliable for dictation applications. The LRS2 (Lip Reading Sentences 2) dataset, introduced in 2017, remains a standard for evaluating lip-reading and automatic speech recognition (ASR) using the word error rate (WER) metric, where lower scores indicate higher accuracy. In 2024, the model Whisper-Flamingo achieved a new state-of-the-art WER of 1.3% on the LRS2 benchmark, surpassing the previous record of 1.5% set in 2023. Because the error rate is already extremely low, significant future improvements appear unlikely, suggesting the benchmark is nearing saturation.

Large language models have become highly proficient at coding, leading to benchmark saturation and the creation of more complex evaluation suites. HumanEval, introduced by OpenAI in 2021, tests coding ability through 164 handwritten programming problems. In early 2025, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (HPT) achieved a perfect 100% Pass@1 score on this benchmark. SWE-bench, launched in October 2023 by researchers from Princeton and the University of Chicago, evaluates software engineering skills using 2,294 real-world GitHub issues and Python repository problems. It features a Lite subset for accessibility and a Verified subset filtered by human annotators. On the SWE-bench Verified set, performance jumped from 4.4% at the end of 2023 to 71.7% for OpenAI’s o3 model in early 2025. To address limitations in testing multi-function invocations and natural-language coding instructions, an international team released BigCodeBench in 2024. This comprehensive benchmark requires LLMs to invoke functions across 139 libraries and 7 domains across 1,140 fine-grained tasks. OpenAI’s o1 model achieved an average Pass@1 score of 35.5 on BigCodeBench’s hard subset. Additionally, the Chatbot Arena Coding filter tracks community perception of model coding skills. As of early 2025, Gemini-Exp-1206 leads the coding arena with an Elo score of 1,369, followed closely by OpenAI’s o1 at 1,361, and Chinese model DeepSeek-V3 at 1,317, representing a 3.8% gap between first and third place.
