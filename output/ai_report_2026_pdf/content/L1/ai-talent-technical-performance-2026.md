# ai-talent-technical-performance-2026

**Parent:** [[content/L2/ai-talent-performance-benchmarks-2026|ai-talent-performance-benchmarks-2026]] — The 2026 AI Index Report highlights a convergence in frontier model performance, with the top four models separated by fewer than 25 Elo points, and a detailed mapping of global AI talent, including the US leading in total authors (220,520) and Switzerland leading per capita (110.45 per 100k).

The 2026 Artificial Intelligence Index Report analyzes the geographic distribution, educational profiles, and mobility of top AI talent, alongside the technical performance and benchmarking of frontier AI models. This synthesis integrates data from 2010 to 2025, focusing on technical outputs such as research, data depositories, and new models, rather than broad labor market indicators.

### AI Talent Distribution and Mobility

#### Geographic and Per Capita Distribution
In 2025, the United States held the largest share of top AI authors and inventors with 220,520 individuals. India followed with 50,460, and Germany with 48,520. A secondary tier of contributors included the United Kingdom (34,370), Canada (31,450), and France (18,820). Further contributors included Australia (14,540), the Netherlands (13,960), Italy (13,230), Brazil (11,100), and Switzerland (9,980), followed by Spain (9,170), Sweden (8,520), Singapore (6,610), Japan (6,280), South Korea (5,960), Israel (5,190), Finland (4,380), Denmark (3,900), Saudi Arabia (3,430), and the United Arab Emirates (3,090).

When measured per 100,000 inhabitants in 2025, Switzerland led the world with 110.45 AI authors and inventors, followed closely by Singapore at 109.51. Other high-ranking nations included Sweden (80.63), Finland (77.61), the Netherlands (77.61), Canada (76.16), and Denmark (65.25). The United States ranked eighth with 64.84, followed by Germany (58.10), Australia (53.43), Israel (52.01), and the United Kingdom (49.64). Lower per capita rankings included the United Arab Emirates (28.38), France (27.46), and Italy (22.43).

### Technical Performance of AI Models

#### Benchmarks and Human Baselines
To evaluate frontier models, the AI Index team utilizes a scaling methodology where the best-performing model of the year is calibrated against a human baseline (100% representing human performance). In 2025, frontier systems reached or exceeded human levels on ImageNet (image classification), SuperGLUE (English language understanding), MMLU (multitask language understanding), MMMU (multimodal reasoning), GPQA Diamond (PhD-level science questions), and AIME (mathematical reasoning). 

However, gaps remain in agent-based multimodal computer use (OSWorld) and autonomous software engineering (SWE-bench Verified). While these are still below human baseline, improvement is accelerating; SWE-bench Verified performance rose from approximately 60% in 2024 to nearly 100% in 2025.

#### Model Convergence and Competition
Model capabilities have converged significantly. As of March 2026, the top tier of the Arena Elo ratings consists of six companies: Anthropic (1,503), xAI (1,495), Google (1,494), OpenAI (1,481), Alibaba (1,449), and DeepSeek (1,424). The top four are separated by fewer than 25 Elo points. Other notable scores include Mistral AI (1,416) and Meta (1,335), the latter having flattened since early 2025.

Competition is no longer focused on raw capability, but on cost, reliability, and real-world usefulness. The gap between closed-weight and open-weight models fluctuates. In May 2023, GPT-4-0314 (closed) outperformed Vicuna-13B (open) by 174 points (15.2%). By August 2024, models like Llama-3.1-405B, Mixtral, and WizardLM narrowed this gap to 7 points (0.5%). As of March 2026, Claude Opus 4.6 (closed, 1,503) leads GLM-5 (open, 1,454) by 49 points (3.4%).

#### U.S. vs. China Performance
The performance gap between the U.S. and China has effectively closed. In February 2025, DeepSeek-R1 (Chinese) briefly matched the top U.S. model. In March 2026, the top U.S. model (Claude Opus 4.6, 1,503) leads the top Chinese model (Dola-Seed-2.0 Preview, 1,464) by 39 points (2.7%). Over the past year, the gap has fluctuated between near parity and low single digits.

#### Benchmark Reliability and "Jagged Intelligence"
Benchmarking is plagued by reliability issues, including error rates reaching 42% on evaluations like GSM8K. Invalid question rates vary from 2% on MMLU Math to 42% on GSM8K. Other issues include benchmark saturation, opacity in prompting techniques, and contamination (e.g., Meta's Llama 4 was criticized, though disputed, for being optimized for benchmarks). Construction errors, such as a lack of replication scripts and statistical significance reporting, are omitted from many benchmarks.

Researchers have observed "jagged intelligence," where models fail at simple tasks despite excelling at complex ones. For example, Gemini Deep Think won a gold medal (35 points) at the 2025 IMO, but on ClockBench, top AI models only read analog clocks correctly 50.6% of the time, compared to 90.1% for humans.

#### Domain-Specific and Agent Performance
- **Professional Domains**: Performance in corporate finance, mortgage processing, tax, and legal reasoning is between 60% and 90%, with top models separated by as little as 3 percentage points.
- **AI Agents**: Accuracy on OSWorld rose from 12% to 66.3%, within 6 percentage points of human performance, though they still fail one in three attempts on structured benchmarks.
- **Robotics**: A massive gap exists between simulation and reality. Success rates for robotic manipulation in software-based simulations (RLBench) are 89.4%, while real household tasks success rates are only 12%.
- **Autonomous Vehicles**: Waymo conducted approximately 450,000 weekly trips in 2025, and China's Apollo Go completed 11 million fully driverless rides (a 175% year-over-year increase).

#### Timeline of Significant Model Releases (2025-2026)
- **DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 20, 2025)**: An LLM using GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) for reinforcement learning without labeled data; its release caused a temporary market value decline of over $1 trillion for U.S. tech stocks.
- **Gemini 2.5 Pro (Mar 25, 2025)**: Multimodal model with 1M tokens context; reached #1 on LMArena and scored ~63.8% on SWE-Bench Verified.
- **Claude Sonnet 4.5 (Sep 29, 2025)**: LLM achieving 61.4% on OSWorld and 77.2%+ on SWE-bench Verified.
- **GPT-5.1 (Nov 12, 2025)**: A faster multimodal model that scores higher on coding and reasoning (~76.3% on SWE-bench Verified vs. ~72.8%).
